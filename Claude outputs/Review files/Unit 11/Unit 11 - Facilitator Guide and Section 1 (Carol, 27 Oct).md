## Unit Intent
Develop executive capability to assess how organisational values become lived culture and how that culture shapes organisational health, then establish the practices required to reinforce healthy organisational behaviour.

## Key learning outcomes
- Diagnose organisational health by evaluating how organisational values and cultural norms influence collective behaviour and strategic performance.
- Shape and reinforce organisational culture by establishing behavioural standards and embedding cultural operating norms that strengthen organisational health.

## Learning Journey
- 1 · Awareness — What: Values, culture and organisational health; Five Observable Health Traits; Organisational Health Pyramid.
- 2 · Intelligence — Why: Translate values into behavioural standards; establish a Cultural Operating Code; embed values in performance measurement and feedback.
- 3 · Extrapolating — Where: Identify cultural vulnerabilities and assess organisational health using the Health Thermometer.
- 4 · Integration — Collective: Align executive expectations and collective responsibility for consistent cultural reinforcement.
- 5 · Application — In Practice: Apply the health assessment and develop targeted cultural reinforcement interventions, including ACE-IT coaching.

## Facilitation Emphasis
Keep the central progression explicit: values establish behavioural principles; culture reflects how these principles are lived; organisational health reflects the resulting collective condition. Use participant organisations as reference points. The executive team presentation takes place only at the final programme panel assessment.

## Section 1 · Awareness — Values, Culture & Organisational Health

- Evaluate how organisational values translate into cultural norms and practices that shape organisational health.
- Diagnose organisational health strengths and vulnerabilities using the Five Observable Health Traits and Organisational Health Pyramid.

1.1 Organisational Values: What We Stand For; 1.2 Organisational Culture: How We Do Things (The Cultural Operating Code); 1.3 Organisational Health: The Resulting Condition (Five Observable Health Traits, Organisational Health Pyramid, Connecting the Five Traits and the Pyramid); Section Closing Insight.

Values table:
- Integrity: Act honestly, communicate transparently and uphold ethical commitments.
- Accountability: Take ownership of responsibilities, honour commitments and report progress accurately.
- Respect: Treat others fairly, listen constructively and recognise different perspectives.
- Excellence: Maintain high standards, address performance gaps and pursue continuous improvement.
- Collaboration: Share information, coordinate efforts and contribute to collective outcomes.

Traits table:
- 1. Minimal Politics: Employees collaborate openly, with limited personal agendas, territorial behaviour and internal competition.
- 2. Minimal Confusion: People understand organisational direction, priorities, responsibilities and performance expectations.
- 3. High Morale: Employees demonstrate commitment, motivation and confidence in their working environment.
- 4. High Productivity: Collective effort is coordinated effectively and translates into meaningful performance outcomes.
- 5. Low Turnover: The organisation retains capable employees through a working environment that supports commitment and contribution.

Pyramid:
- Trust — The Foundation: Trust forms the foundation of organisational health. It develops through consistent leadership behaviour, including keeping commitments, communicating transparently, acknowledging mistakes and treating people fairly. When trust is established, employees develop confidence in their leaders and colleagues. They become more willing to communicate openly, raise concerns and contribute to collective objectives. Leadership implication: Organisational health begins with the trust established through consistent leadership conduct.
- Experiences — Shaped by Trust: Employees experience organisational culture through everyday interactions, meetings, performance conversations, leadership decisions and recognition practices. These experiences communicate what the organisation genuinely values and the behaviours it considers acceptable. For example, the way leaders respond to mistakes influences whether employees experience the organisation as supportive of learning or inclined towards blame. Leadership implication: Everyday organisational experiences reinforce or weaken the values leadership seeks to establish.
- Beliefs — Formed by Experiences: Through repeated experiences, employees develop beliefs about how the organisation operates. They form assumptions about whether leaders listen, whether commitments matter, whether effort is recognised and whether honest communication is encouraged. Over time, these beliefs influence how employees interpret organisational expectations and their own responsibilities. Leadership implication: Repeated organisational experiences establish the beliefs that influence collective behaviour.
- Actions — Driven by Beliefs: Employees’ beliefs influence how they behave and contribute to organisational performance. When employees believe their contributions are valued, they are more likely to share ideas, collaborate and take initiative. When they believe accountability is consistently reinforced, they are more likely to take ownership of responsibilities and honour commitments. Leadership implication: The beliefs established through organisational culture influence the behaviours employees demonstrate.
- Results — The Visible Traits: The actions employees consistently demonstrate produce collective organisational outcomes. These outcomes become visible through the Five Observable Health Traits: Minimal Politics, Minimal Confusion, High Morale, High Productivity and Low Turnover. At the apex of the pyramid, organisational results reflect the cumulative influence of trust, experiences, beliefs and actions. A commitment to continuous improvement helps sustain these results as the organisation evolves. Leadership implication: The visible condition of organisational health reflects the underlying cultural experiences and behaviours established throughout the organisation.

Closing insight: Values establish the principles that guide organisational behaviour. Culture reflects how those principles become established ways of working. Organisational health reveals how effectively those values are lived through the culture that has developed.
