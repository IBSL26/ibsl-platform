# Cross Leadership Clog Ownership

Building the collective discipline of normalising the unclog, where detecting and clearing performance friction becomes a shared leadership habit.

## Section learning outcomes

-   Mobilise leaders across functions to restore performance momentum together.

-   Establish a shared leadership rhythm for surfacing and resolving friction across people and organisational performance.

## Facilitator Guidance Integration

The central shift is from “my clog” to “our clog”. A performance blockage may surface in one function while the organisational condition sustaining it sits in another. The leadership team, acting as the collective intelligence for execution discipline, must own the diagnostic conversation and response together.

Emphasise the “see something, say something” principle: surfacing friction in another executive domain can feel politically risky, yet early identification is an act of shared leadership stewardship. Use Review–Realign–Restore as the established S2R discipline for acting on what leaders surface. REALIGN must explicitly consider the implications for both people performance and organisational performance.

Work through the manufacturing example and then guide participants to apply the same process to their capstone organisation. Keep the Seven Unclogging Levers as the intervention mechanism within RESTORE.

## 4.1 — Normalising the Unclog From Crisis to Habit

Performance friction is a normal feature of organisational life. Strategic priorities shift, resources become constrained, dependencies increase and existing operating arrangements may no longer support the required results. Without a shared leadership discipline, these problems are discussed informally, addressed within silos or left unresolved across successive performance reviews.

Normalising the unclog means making the early detection and removal of performance constraints part of how the executive team operates. Leaders raise concerns through evidence, examine the contributing conditions together and agree on an accountable response. Surfacing friction is treated as a contribution to organisational learning and performance, including when the condition involves another executive portfolio.

The leadership team follows a consistent path: REVIEW the performance evidence and confirm the clog; REALIGN executive ownership and the changes required across people and organisational performance; RESTORE by activating the relevant Seven Unclogging Lever and verifying recovery.

**Collective leadership principle.** Every executive is responsible for making emerging friction visible. The leaders who control the contributing conditions must act together to remove it, with clear individual ownership for each agreed commitment.

## 4.2 — REVIEW Establish the Performance Reality

REVIEW establishes a shared, evidence-based understanding of the result falling short and the organisational condition contributing to it. Leaders use the Three Performance Lines of Sight to distinguish the outcome from the behaviours observed and the conditions causing or reinforcing them.

1\. Performance Outcome — Confirm the Key Result or KPI that is underperforming and establish the actual gap.

2\. Performance Behaviour — Identify what individuals or teams are doing, delaying or unable to do that contributes to the gap.

3\. Performance Condition — Test the evidence against the Seven Clogs and identify the condition sustaining the behaviour.

The review conversation must allow leaders to surface performance friction across functional boundaries. Concerns should be examined through observable evidence, including where the condition may sit within another executive portfolio.

**REVIEW is complete when** the executive team agrees on the performance gap, the supporting evidence and the clog requiring intervention.

## 4.3 — REALIGN Establish Collective Executive Ownership

Once a performance clog has been confirmed, the executive team must determine which organisational condition needs to change and which leaders have the authority and responsibility to act. A clog may be experienced in one function but sustained by a decision or operating arrangement in another.

### Three Leadership Decisions

1\. What must change? Identify the specific organisational arrangement, practice or decision sustaining the clog.

2\. Who must act together? Appoint one coordinating executive owner and name the contributing executives needed to resolve the condition.

3\. What must be agreed? Establish the decisions, responsibilities, resources and timelines required for the intervention.

Before finalising the response, leaders consider the implications for both dimensions of performance:

**People performance.** What must change in expectations, authority, capabilities, behaviours or support so individuals and teams can deliver their contributions?

**Organisational performance.** What must change in structures, processes, systems, resources, decision rights or governance so the enterprise can produce the intended result?

The agreed intervention should address the confirmed organisational condition while enabling the people responsible for delivery to perform effectively.

Every executive has a responsibility to recognise and surface performance friction. Collective ownership is supported by clear individual accountability: one leader coordinates the resolution, while other executives remain accountable for their agreed contributions.

**REALIGN is complete when** the relevant leaders have agreed what must change, who will act and what each executive must deliver.

## 4.4 — RESTORE Activate the Seven Unclogging Levers

RESTORE converts the agreed leadership response into organisational action. The Seven Unclogging Levers provide the established S2R interventions for addressing the conditions represented by the Seven Performance Clogs. The executive team selects the relevant lever, implements the agreed changes and reviews evidence of recovery.

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Established lever**   **Purpose**             **Best applied to**
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  01 — Standardise        Ensure Consistency      Structural Misfit; Misaligned Metrics & Incentives; Technology / Infrastructure Lag

  02 — Streamline         Increase Speed          Decision Bottlenecks; Structural Misfit

  03 — Clarify            Eliminate Confusion     Decision Bottlenecks; Structural Misfit; Leadership Avoidance

  04 — Upgrade            Remove Bottlenecks      Technology / Infrastructure Lag; Capability Gaps

  05 — Reinforce          Strengthen Behaviour    Capability Gaps; Cultural Resistance

  06 — Realign            Focus on Results        Misaligned Metrics & Incentives

  07 — Rebuild Trust      Restore Credibility     Cultural Resistance; Leadership Avoidance
  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These are the established Seven Strategic Unclogging Levers introduced in Section 2. The executive team chooses a lever according to the confirmed condition and the specific obstruction to be removed. The S2R REALIGN stage is the collective decision and ownership step; Lever 06 — Realign is the distinct intervention used to adjust metrics and rewards.

1\. Select the relevant Unclogging Lever. Choose the intervention that addresses the confirmed clog.

2\. Activate the intervention. Implement the decisions and changes agreed during REALIGN.

3\. Verify performance recovery. Confirm that the obstruction is reducing and that the affected performance measures are moving in the intended direction.

Removing a clog requires evidence that the organisational condition has changed. Sustained improvement is verified through the normal performance review rhythm.

## 4.5 — Worked Example Review Realign Restore

**Enterprise Objective.** Improve production reliability and on-time customer delivery.

**Enterprise Key Result.** Increase customer orders delivered on time from 72% to 95% by year-end.

Performance figures are illustrative. The executive team notices that production interruptions are contributing to late deliveries.

### Step 1 — REVIEW Confirm the Performance Clog

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Line of sight**                   **Diagnostic finding**
  ----------------------------------- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Performance Outcome                 Only 72% of customer orders are delivered on time, against the 95% target.

  Performance Behaviour               Maintenance managers wait for approval before ordering routine replacement parts. Equipment repairs and production schedules are delayed.

  Performance Condition               Financial approval requirements place routine maintenance decisions at senior management level, slowing necessary purchases.
  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Identified clog.** Decision Bottlenecks.

The team confirms that avoidable approval delays contribute to maintenance downtime and late customer deliveries.

### Step 2 — REALIGN Agree on Collective Ownership

The CFO and COO agree that routine maintenance decisions must be faster while appropriate financial controls remain in place.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Leadership decision**             **Agreed response**
  ----------------------------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  What must change?                   Approval authority for routine maintenance expenditure.

  Who coordinates?                    CFO — revise financial approval arrangements.

  Who contributes?                    COO — define operational requirements and ensure adoption.

  What must be agreed?                Delegated thresholds, control requirements, responsibilities and an implementation date.
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**People performance implication.** Maintenance managers gain clear authority to purchase approved routine replacement parts and can act promptly within defined controls.

**Organisational performance implication.** The approval system preserves financial oversight while enabling reliable equipment maintenance and uninterrupted production flow.

### Step 3 — RESTORE Activate the Unclogging Lever

**Selected lever.** Lever 02 — Streamline \| Increase Speed.

Streamline simplifies workflows and removes approval layers that add no value to execution. It is appropriate because the confirmed clog is Decision Bottlenecks, and routine maintenance purchases are delayed by unnecessary approval steps.

**Activation.** The CFO and COO implement the following changes:

1\. Identify approval steps that cause avoidable delays to routine maintenance purchases.

2\. Remove redundant approvals and simplify the routine purchase workflow.

3\. Retain appropriate controls and senior approval for exceptional or higher-risk expenditure.

4\. Communicate and implement the streamlined process with maintenance and operational teams.

**People performance after Streamline.** Maintenance managers can obtain routine replacement parts promptly and fulfil their responsibilities within clear approval limits, without repeated unnecessary escalation.

**Organisational performance after Streamline.** Reduced approval time lowers maintenance downtime, supports production continuity and helps restore on-time customer delivery while preserving financial control.

**Verification.** At the agreed executive review, examine:

-   Routine maintenance approval turnaround time.

-   Equipment downtime caused by delayed replacement parts.

-   On-time customer delivery performance.

-   Compliance with the revised financial controls.

The intervention is working when maintenance managers can exercise the revised authority confidently, routine decisions are made faster, approval-related downtime falls and customer delivery performance begins to recover without weakening financial control.

### Completed Unclogging Record

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **S2R discipline**                  **Leadership record**
  ----------------------------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  REVIEW                              Decision Bottlenecks are contributing to maintenance and production delays.

  REALIGN                             CFO coordinates; COO contributes. Both agree on revised approval authority and controls.

  RESTORE                             Activate Lever 02 — Streamline and verify improvement through approval time, downtime, delivery and financial-control evidence.
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Facilitator Closing Note Integration

Close by reinforcing both the shared leadership habit and the established S2R discipline. Leaders must make emerging friction visible and respond collectively. REVIEW establishes the performance reality. REALIGN establishes collective leadership responsibility and the changes required for people and organisational performance. RESTORE activates the relevant intervention and verifies recovery.

The goal is to make the removal of performance clogs a consistent part of executive review, decision-making and follow-through. Section 5 applies this integrated discipline to the participants’ strategic results.
