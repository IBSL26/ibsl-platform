Module 4 · Grounding · Unit 10

ESRG Alignment

Grounding · Pillar 3 of DIG

Grounding establishes the organisational capabilities and healthy operating status required to anchor and sustain strategic performance. It considers how the enterprise is configured to deliver results, the organisational health that sustains performance, and how both are leveraged over time.

The Grounding learning journey progresses from Unit 10 — ESRG Alignment (organisational operating environment), to Unit 11 — Culture Reinforcement & Organisational Health (culture), and then to Unit 12 — Sustaining Performance, which leverages both to sustain high performance.

Module learning outcomes Configure organisational operating conditions to support strategic priorities and enable consistent enterprise performance. Shape organisational culture by establishing and embedding the conditions and practices that strengthen organisational health. Integrate organisational capabilities and cultural practices into a coherent operating environment that supports sustained high performance. Institutionalise organisational performance sustainability through mechanisms that preserve strategic alignment, strengthen adaptability and maintain performance continuity.
Key learning outcomes Design an ESRG-aligned organisational operating environment by establishing the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance requirements necessary to deliver strategic commitments. Develop targeted organisational alignment interventions that address operating constraints and strengthen the organisation’s capacity for consistent strategic delivery.

Section 1 · Awareness — What

What operating conditions must support strategic delivery?

Specifying the organisational capability one Objective requires, domain by domain.

Section learning outcomes
  • Specify the essential organisational operating requirements associated with a selected Objective.
  • Assign each requirement to the appropriate ESRG domain and component, explaining its direct contribution to the intended result.
ESRG Alignment — The Grounding Question

In Unit 6 you met ESRG as the Organisational Operating System: Environment (FIT), Systems (SIP), Resources (AIR) and Governance (PAR), each with three components. There, ESRG answered a performance management question: when results fall short, does the cause lie in people’s performance or in the operating conditions around them?

Unit 10 builds on that foundation. It uses ESRG to ground performance at the level of the Objective: before demand arrives, leaders specify what each component must provide for an Objective to be delivered, and build that capability deliberately. Key Results are reviewed through the year in EPIC (Unit 8); the ESRG review sits one level above them, on the Objective those Key Results serve. Parts 1.1 to 1.4 take each domain in turn and set out, component by component, what must be established for strategic delivery. The executive team tests these requirements once a year, at the ESRG Annual Scan of each Objective.

1.1 — Environment | FIT

Environment concerns the suitability and reliability of the physical and technological setting in which organisational operations take place.

FITComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
FFacilitiesWhether operating locations and physical facilities are functional and suited to the planned activity.
IInfrastructureWhether essential utilities, connectivity and supporting infrastructure can reliably carry operational demand.
TTechnologyWhether the technological operating environment is available, reliable and fit for the required activity.

Diagnostic question: Are our facilities, infrastructure and technology fit for the strategic requirement?

Illustration: A production expansion depends on stable electrical supply. Repeated outages prevent the additional production capacity from being used consistently.

1.2 — Systems | SIP

Systems concerns how operational work is defined, connected and performed efficiently across the enterprise.

SIPComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
SStandardisationWhether recurring operational processes are clearly defined and consistently applied.
IIntegrationWhether systems and process handoffs connect across functions without avoidable breaks.
PProcess EfficiencyWhether the required output can be delivered without unnecessary steps, delay, duplication or waste.

Diagnostic question: Are our operational processes standardised, integrated and efficient?

Illustration: Customer onboarding follows a documented process, but disconnected sales and finance platforms require repeated manual entry, slowing completion.

1.3 — Resources | AIR

Resources concerns the availability, funding and productive deployment of the organisation’s operational assets.

AIRComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
AAsset AvailabilityWhether sufficient productive equipment and other operating assets are available for the required output.
IInvestmentWhether financial provision is adequate for the strategic and operational commitment.
RResource UtilisationWhether existing assets and funding are allocated and used productively against priorities.

Diagnostic question: Are our assets and financial resources sufficient and effectively utilised?

Illustration: A logistics operation has enough vehicles, but uneven deployment leaves some idle while others are overused, reducing delivery reliability.

1.4 — Governance | PAR

Governance concerns the institutional arrangements through which operational decisions are guided, authorised and exposed risks managed.

PARComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
PPolicies & ProceduresWhether the necessary institutional policies and procedures are established and suitable for the decisions and operations required.
AAuthorityWhether decision rights, delegated authority and approval pathways support timely decisions.
RRisk ManagementWhether material risks to the strategic commitment are identified, assessed, mitigated and monitored.

Diagnostic question: Is our organisational governance up to PAR?

Illustration: Funding for expansion has been approved, yet avoidable approval levels delay the release of expenditure and postpone implementation.

Section 2 · Intelligence — Why

The ESRG Ignition Point: How Each Domain Enables Execution

The four execution-enabling capabilities, and the ESRG Annual Scan through which an executive team reads its operating conditions at three levels of intelligence.

Section learning outcomes
  • Translate strategic performance requirements into functional operating capabilities through the four ESRG execution enablers.
  • Determine the response each operating-condition reading calls for, from the function to the enterprise.
2.1 — The ESRG Ignition Point

The ESRG Ignition Point is reached when organisational operating conditions are sufficiently aligned to support the performance demands of the strategy. The four domains make distinct, interdependent contributions to execution:

ESRG domainExecution-enabling capabilityWhat it makes possible
Environment · FITOperational ReadinessProvides a dependable operating setting: facilities accommodate planned activity; essential infrastructure sustains continuity; and technology has the functionality, reliability and capacity to handle strategic demand. This reduces interruptions and protects service or production availability.
Systems · SIPOperational FlowEnables work to progress predictably from initiation to completion: standardised processes create consistency, integrated workflows support handovers across functions, and efficient process design removes avoidable steps, duplication and waiting time. This improves throughput and turnaround time.
Resources · AIRDelivery CapacityMakes the required scale of delivery achievable: productive assets are available when needed, financial investment supports strategic priorities, and existing resources are deployed where demand is greatest. This prevents capacity shortages and avoidable underutilisation.
Governance · PARDecision ResponsivenessAllows organisational action to proceed with clarity and control: policies and procedures guide consistent operations, authority arrangements enable decisions at the right level and time, and risks are identified and managed. This reduces approval bottlenecks while protecting sound decisions.

Ignition principle: A strategic commitment can be delivered consistently only when the operating conditions it depends upon can meet its performance demands.

Illustration: A customer service function has reliable technology and adequate assets, but disconnected handovers delay case resolution. Its constraint is Operational Flow (SIP). Additional equipment would not directly resolve the delay.

Reflect: Which of the four capabilities is most critical to the Objective your function serves, and what would happen if it were unable to meet the required demand?
2.2 — Three Dimensions of Collective ESRG Intelligence

The ESRG Ignition Point in 2.1 is reached for the enterprise only when each execution-enabling capability holds across every function an Objective depends on. Leaders test this once a year through the ESRG Annual Scan (2.3), which moves through three levels of intelligence. Each level works from the output of the level before it:

Functional Intelligence

Each function averages its members’ scores into one score for each component, and the score sets its response.

Cross-Functional Intelligence

The functions place their interventions side by side and decide whether each one is shared, dependent or standalone.

Enterprise Intelligence

The executive team weighs every intervention against its impact on enterprise results and sets the order of delivery.

2.3 — The ESRG Annual Scan
2.3.1  What the scan is

The ESRG Annual Scan is the leadership team’s yearly reading of the operating conditions that ground performance. Once a year, each function scores the components of Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance as they stand in its own environment, at Objective level, using the Functional ESRG Performance Scanner taught through the worked example in Section 4. Key Results are reviewed through EPIC across the year. The executive team then brings the functional readings together and agrees the interventions that will carry the organisation’s performance through the year ahead.

2.3.2  Scoring points

Each member scores each component on their own, from the evidence of how it performs in practice, on a 1–5 scale:

ScoreRatingMeaning
1Critical constraintCondition materially prevents required performance.
2Significant gapCondition regularly disrupts or limits performance.
3Partially effectiveSupports basic operations but does not consistently meet strategic demand.
4EffectiveReliably supports current strategic performance requirements.
5Fully alignedReliably supports requirements and anticipated strategic demand.
2.3.3  The ESRG Intelligence Key

The scan consolidates the members’ scores: a component’s score is the average of the members’ scores. The ESRG Intelligence Key reads that score in three bands, and each band sets the response:

ScoreReadingResponse
1.0–1.9Critical constraint: the condition prevents the function from deliveringEscalate: the function cannot resolve it with its own members, authority or budget; it goes to the executive team as a candidate enterprise priority, with a named lead from the function.
2.0–3.9Significant gap or partially effective: the condition disrupts delivery or falls short of strategic demandCoordinate: the function resolves it with its own members, authority and budget: one response, led by one accountable person from the function, delivered within the function’s plan for the year.
4.0–5.0Effective or fully aligned: the condition carries the strategic demandProtect: no intervention; the function keeps the condition at this level and watches for any drop at the next scan.
2.3.4  The decision point at each level of intelligence

Each level of intelligence makes one decision and passes its result to the next level.

Functional Intelligence. The function asks of each component: can we resolve this condition with our own members, authority and budget? The ESRG Intelligence Key answers from the score, and a score of 4.0 or above is protected. For every component below 4.0, the function states the situation behind the score: what is happening, and how it affects the Objective or its Key Results. It then sets the intervention and its lead. A Coordinate intervention stays inside the function: its own members deliver it, led by one accountable person from the function. An Escalate intervention names the constraint the function cannot resolve alone, with a lead from the function, and goes on to the executive team. The situations give the next two levels the specifics they act on.

Cross-Functional Intelligence. The functions place their interventions side by side, escalated constraints included, and ask of each one: is it shared, dependent or standalone? Each function’s scores reflect its own operating conditions, so a Finance reading and a People reading will differ; the functions compare their interventions. Each intervention takes one of three results:

  • Combine: the same fix appears in more than one function, so it becomes one intervention with one lead, funded once.
  • Align: one function’s delivery relies on a capability another function supplies or controls, so the functions agree the shared operating requirement, the order of action and what each commits to the other.
  • Retain: the intervention is independent. It stays with its function, unchanged, under the function’s own lead.

Enterprise Intelligence. The executive team asks: which interventions most advance enterprise results, and in what order are they delivered? It prioritises every escalated constraint, combined intervention and aligned dependency against the Objective and its Key Results, and names the lead executive for each. Retained Coordinate interventions stay function-led: the ESRG Alignment Key lists them under the priorities, so the executive team sees all the work for the year.

2.4 — The Risk of Absence and the Benefits
The risk of absence
  • Strategy outruns its conditions: commitments are approved without checking that the facilities, systems, assets and decision rights can carry them.
  • Clogs keep recurring: the same Key Results slow down quarter after quarter because the underlying condition is never built. Leaders clear the symptoms without fixing the cause.
  • Duplicated spend: functions fund separate fixes for the same constraint, such as two system upgrades that should be one integration.
  • Hidden dependencies: one function promises delivery that depends on another function’s capacity, and nobody agrees who commits what.
  • Effort spread thin: without prioritising by enterprise impact, resources go to the loudest constraint, and the constraint holding back results stays in place.
The benefits
  • Operating conditions built ahead of demand, so strategic commitments are delivered consistently.
  • One annual reading of operating conditions, so they are not rescored every quarter.
  • Critical constraints escalated to the executive team early, each with a named lead.
  • One intervention where functions would otherwise fund several, with clear commitments between functions.
  • A prioritised ESRG Alignment Key that directs resources to the interventions with the greatest impact on enterprise results.

Where Do Executive Biases Distort ESRG Operating Capabilities?

Section 4 · Integration — Collective

How Does the Executive Team Create Collective Intelligence for ESRG Alignment?

A worked example of the ESRG Annual Scan in four steps: the members of each function score its operating conditions, each function reads its scores, the functions combine, align or retain each intervention, and the executive team prioritises the interventions.

Section learning outcomes
  • Integrate the functional ESRG readings of an executive team into one coherent set of interventions that strengthens cross-functional delivery.
  • Prioritise operating-condition interventions by their impact on enterprise results.

The worked example: One illustrative company carries out its ESRG Annual Scan; the company and its figures come from no real organisation. Its Objective is to be the most reliable supplier in its market. One of its Key Results, fulfilling customer orders within five working days, is reviewed through the year in EPIC; once a year, the executive team scans ESRG for the Objective. The example follows two of the functions that carry the Objective, Operations and Commercial, each scored by three of its members.

The scan runs in four steps: Step 1, the members of each function score its operating conditions (4.1); Step 2, each function reads its scores, states the situation behind each score below 4.0 and agrees its interventions (4.2, Functional Intelligence); Step 3, the functions decide whether each intervention is combined, aligned or retained (4.3, Cross-Functional Intelligence); Step 4, the executive team prioritises the interventions by their impact on enterprise results (4.4, Enterprise Intelligence).

The competent leader configures the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance that strategy needs. The organisation gains the capacity to deliver its commitments consistently.

The unit concept

A strategic commitment can be delivered consistently only when the operating conditions it depends on can meet its performance demands. ESRG Alignment grounds organisational performance at the level of the Objective: before demand arrives, leaders specify what each component of the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance must provide, test it once a year in the ESRG Annual Scan, and build the capability deliberately. Each domain enables execution through one capability: Operational Readiness, Operational Flow, Delivery Capacity and Decision Responsiveness. Each function reads these capabilities from its own position: its members’ scores are averaged into one score per component and the ESRG Intelligence Key sets its response, the functions combine, align or retain each intervention, and the executive team prioritises the interventions by their impact on enterprise results.

Infrastructure: utilities, connectivity and supporting infrastructure reliably carry operational demand. 2. The ESRG Ignition Point

The Ignition Point is reached when operating conditions are aligned enough to support the performance demands of the strategy. Each domain makes a distinct, interdependent contribution:

DomainExecution-enabling capabilityWhat it makes possible
Environment · FITOperational ReadinessA dependable operating setting that reduces interruptions and protects service or production availability
Systems · SIPOperational FlowWork that progresses predictably from initiation to completion, improving throughput and turnaround
Resources · AIRDelivery CapacityThe required scale of delivery, preventing capacity shortages and avoidable underutilisation
Governance · PARDecision ResponsivenessAction that proceeds with clarity and control, reducing approval bottlenecks while protecting sound decisions

Ignition principle: a strategic commitment can be delivered consistently only when the operating conditions it depends on can meet its performance demands. An intervention must address the capability that is actually constraining delivery.

3. The three levels of intelligence in the ESRG Annual Scan

The ESRG Annual Scan is the leadership team’s yearly reading of the operating conditions that ground performance, at Objective level; Key Results are reviewed through EPIC across the year. Each function scores the components as they stand in its own environment; the executive team then brings the functional readings together and agrees the interventions for the year ahead.

The ESRG Intelligence Key. The scan consolidates the members’ scores: a component’s score is the average of the members’ scores, read in three bands.

A low score does not by itself prove the cause: the evidence is tested before the response is agreed.

4. The ESRG Annual Scan in four steps

  1. The members of each function score its operating conditions. Each member scores the 12 components for the Objective on their own, from evidence of how policies, systems and plans perform in practice, on a 1–5 scale: 1 Critical constraint (materially prevents required performance); 2 Significant gap (regularly disrupts or limits performance); 3 Partially effective (supports basic operations, not strategic demand); 4 Effective (reliably supports current requirements); 5 Fully aligned (supports current and anticipated demand).
  2. Each function reads its scores (Functional Intelligence). The function averages its members’ scores into one score for each component, and the ESRG Intelligence Key sets the response: Escalate at 1.0–1.9, Coordinate at 2.0–3.9, Protect at 4.0–5.0. For every component below 4.0, the function states the situation behind the score, the intervention and its lead.
  3. The functions combine, align or retain (Cross-Functional Intelligence). Each intervention is combined where the same fix appears in more than one function, aligned where one function relies on a capability another supplies or controls (the functions agree the shared operating requirement, the order of action and what each commits to the other), or retained by its function where it is independent.
  4. The executive team builds its ESRG Alignment Key (Enterprise Intelligence). It prioritises the escalated constraints, combined interventions and aligned dependencies by their impact on enterprise results and names the lead executive for each; retained interventions are listed as function-led. The Key is the commitment for the year.
