Module 4 · Grounding · Unit 12

Sustaining Performance

Grounding · Pillar 3 of DIG

Grounding establishes the organisational capabilities and healthy operating status required to anchor and sustain strategic performance. It considers how the enterprise is configured to deliver results, the organisational health that sustains performance, and how both are leveraged over time.

The Grounding learning journey progresses from Unit 10 — ESRG Alignment (organisational operating environment), to Unit 11 — Culture Reinforcement & Organisational Health (culture), and then to this unit, which leverages both to sustain high performance.

Module learning outcomes Configure organisational operating conditions to support strategic priorities and enable consistent enterprise performance. Shape organisational culture by establishing and embedding the conditions and practices that strengthen organisational health. Integrate organisational capabilities and cultural practices into a coherent operating environment that supports sustained high performance. Institutionalise organisational performance sustainability through mechanisms that preserve strategic alignment, strengthen adaptability and maintain performance continuity.
Key learning outcomes Distinguish short-term performance attainment from sustained institutional capability. Maintain continuous sight of strategic, operational and behavioural performance across people and organisational performance. Recognise the sources of performance drift and the early erosion of strategic coherence. Exercise forward-looking executive judgement that protects organisational value and long-term performance.

Session Overview

Unit intent, learning journey, key facilitation questions and tone for the full unit.

◆ Unit Intent

Unit 12 answers the closing question of the programme: how does a leadership team hold results once it has achieved them? Results drift back towards average under Performance Gravity, and the drift shows first on one of the Three Lines of Sight. The unit teaches a quarterly reinforcement of both sides of performance.

People performance is read on the Behavioural line through the Organisational Health Thermometer (Unit 11), and restored through Review–Realign–Restore (Unit 8). Organisational performance is read on the Strategic and Operational lines through EPIC (Unit 8): at quarter end the executive team gathers the quarter’s open Restore decisions and the open ESRG Alignment Key interventions (Unit 10), gives each its line of sight and RAG, and unblocks every Red decision through Strategic Unclogging (Unit 9).

Each section carries both sides one step further:

◆ Key Facilitation Questions
  • “Think of a result your organisation achieved and then lost. Which line of sight showed the drift first, and who was watching it?”
  • “When your executive team agrees a recovery, who checks at quarter end that it was carried through?”
  • “Which norm in your organisation holds only when leaders are watching?”
◆ Tone & Watch Points
  • Nothing here is new to the participants. The Health Thermometer and the Cultural Operating Code come from Unit 11, EPIC and Review–Realign–Restore from Unit 8, the ESRG Alignment Key from Unit 10, and Strategic Unclogging from Unit 9. Present Unit 12 as the quarterly rhythm that keeps them working.
  • The results are already theirs. Each group conducted an EPIC in Unit 8, built its ESRG Alignment Key in Unit 10 and its Cultural Operating Code in Unit 11; Section 5 brings that work onto the page.
  • Watch for results-only reading. Leaders who read only the Key Result see drift last. Bring the conversation back to the health reading and the open Restore decisions each time it settles on the result alone.
  • Keep Restore decisions specific. Each one names the action, the owner and the date. Guide participants from general intentions to named owners and dates.
  • Treat a Red decision as a block to clear. Guide the room to name what is holding it and who clears it.
Section 1 · Awareness — What

What Sustains Performance

Performance Gravity, the Three Lines of Sight that show it, and the quarterly reinforcement of people and organisational performance that holds results against it.

Section learning outcomes
  • Recognise the force that pulls achieved results back towards average.
  • Identify the leadership practices that hold people and organisational performance against that pull.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Awareness

Open with the question of the unit: once a result is achieved, what holds it? Teach 1.1 and 1.2 first, then return to the room with the opening question in the Key Facilitation Questions.

In 1.3, connect the thermometer reading to Review, the norm behind each priority trait to Realign, and the corrective action to Restore. Participants met each in Units 8 and 11.

In 1.4, teach the four steps in order, starting with the two sources of decisions in Step 1, and show the RAG key before any example. Close with 1.5: the people reading and the EPIC reinforcement feed each other.

1.1 — Performance Gravity

Achieving a result and holding it are two separate leadership tasks. The strategy, the contributions and the performance system built across this programme produce results. Once achieved, those results come under a steady pull back towards the organisation’s historical average. This pull is Performance Gravity.

Performance Gravity works quietly. Results often remain acceptable for several cycles while the conditions and behaviours that produced them weaken underneath. By the time the decline shows in the result, the cost of correcting it is far higher than the cost of noticing it early.

Sustaining performance is the leadership discipline of seeing that pull early and responding to it every quarter, for people performance and for organisational performance.

1.2 — The Three Lines of Sight
Strategic · Operational · Behavioural

Performance Gravity shows first as drift on one of three lines of sight. The executive team keeps all three in view each quarter, reading each from a result it already produces.

1.4 — Sustaining Organisational Performance
The Strategic and Operational lines of sight · Quarterly EPIC Reinforcement

Organisational performance is the result of the Organisational Operating System (Unit 6). Through the quarter, each EPIC agrees a Restore decision for every gap. At quarter end, the executive team reinforces those decisions, so that no recovery is left open and the Key Result holds. The same review follows through the interventions in the ESRG Alignment Key from the annual scan (Unit 10) until each is complete.

From people to organisation. A cause found in the people reading that lies in an organisational condition goes to EPIC as a Restore decision.

At each quarter end, the executive team sees both sides of performance in one view.

2.1 — Why Performance Drifts
Select each line to open it

Performance Gravity works through a different mechanism on each line of sight.

Strategic line · Momentum Illusion

The market, customers and competitors keep moving after the strategy is set. The organisation continues running the approach that produced its results, and the results stay acceptable for a while because momentum carries them forward. The Key Result keeps meeting its waypoints while the Success in Practice it serves moves further away.

How it shows: waypoints met with growing effort; customers referring to alternatives that did not exist when the strategy was set; win rates and renewals moving slightly in the wrong direction.

Operational line · Complexity Creep

Every change in regulation, cost or technology brings a structural response: a new approval, a new report, a new process. Each response made sense when it was created. Together they leave the organisation heavier and slower than its current environment requires, and the conditions named in the Contribution Map begin to constrain the people’s tasks.

How it shows: approvals and processes no one can explain the current purpose of; manual workarounds; budget structures built for conditions that have since changed.

Behavioural line · Execution Regression

Every organisation carries habits, norms and assumptions reinforced over years. Once attention moves away from a new practice, these habits pull behaviour back towards the historical pattern. Review cadences loosen, escalation slows and standards return to earlier levels, often without anyone deciding to relax them.

How it shows: reviews shortened or postponed; issues carried across several cycles; accountability applied unevenly; agreed behaviours visible only when leaders are watching.

Execution Regression weakens the organisation’s ability to respond on the other two lines, because the routines that detect drift are the first to loosen.

Unit Summary
Unit 12 synthesis · S2R® Programme Close
COMPETENCE DEVELOPED
Sustainable Performance

The competent leader builds lasting institutional capability that outlives short-term results. They keep enterprise performance in continuous sight and act early when it begins to drift.

Demonstrable skills

  1. Maintain the Three Lines of Sight
  2. Detect Performance Gravity and early drift
  3. Reinforce recovery across people and organisational performance

The unit concept

Achieving a result and holding it are two separate leadership tasks. Once achieved, results come under Performance Gravity: a steady pull back towards the historical average, working quietly while the conditions and behaviours behind the result weaken. Gravity shows first as drift on one of three lines of sight: the Behavioural line, read through the Organisational Health Thermometer for people performance, and the Strategic and Operational lines, read through EPIC for organisational performance. At each quarter end, the executive team reads the thermometer and restores the norms behind the lowest-scoring traits through Review–Realign–Restore. It gathers the quarter’s open EPIC Restore decisions and ESRG Alignment Key interventions, tags each with its line of sight and RAG, and unblocks every Red decision. The two readings feed each other, and together they hold the result into the next quarter.

3. Sustaining people performance

Review: the enterprise and functional health scores in their bands (Healthy & Thriving 80–100; Stable but Uneven 60–79; Caution Zone 40–59; Unhealthy 20–39), and the lowest-scoring traits this quarter as the priority traits. Realign: trace each priority trait to its norm (Minimal Politics: raising issues, sharing information and resolving conflict openly; Minimal Confusion: an explicit expected behaviour for every value, applied the same way by every leader; High Morale: consistent recognition of behaviour that matches the norms; High Productivity: ownership, coordination and follow-through; Low Turnover: fair consequences applied consistently), and find the cause through the Accountability Loop: Ownership, Capability, or Role or structural constraint. Restore: each owner commits to the corrective action and a review date; the next thermometer is the next Review.

4. Sustaining organisational performance

Closed decisions and completed interventions are Green and stay off the table. Each Red decision is unblocked through Strategic Unclogging, with its owner confirmed and a new date set.
