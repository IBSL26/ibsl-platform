Unit 3 — Session Overview

Use this tab to prepare before each section. It contains the unit intent, key facilitation questions, and tone and watch points for the full unit.

◆ Unit Intent

Unit 3 converts the Success in Practice narrative into a measurable execution architecture. The KISS reflection examines current practice honestly before any target is set; OKRs then express the enterprise priorities that reflection reveals.

The six-step synthesis is the core of the unit. It moves executives from functional objectives to enterprise priorities judged on strategic merit.

◆ Key Facilitation Questions
  • “Which current practice in your organisation most advances the SiP, and which most constrains it?”
  • “Look at your draft Key Result. Does it measure a result or an activity?”
  • “Which OKR hot zone describes how you instinctively set objectives? What would an enterprise-level Key Result look like instead?”
  • “If the functional labels were removed from the proposed OKRs, which would the team still choose?”
◆ Tone & Watch Points

Watch for: a rush to OKRs before the KISS reflection is complete. The reflection is the bridge; hold the sequence.

Watch for: Key Results written as activities such as “launch” or “implement”. Apply the formula: verb of change + metric + from X to Y + by deadline.

Watch for: advocacy for functional OKRs during synthesis. Use de-labelling so each proposal is judged on its merit.

Section 1 · Awareness — What

What is KISS Framing & OKR Definition?

Interrogative Anchor · Before defining Objectives and Key Results, the leadership team must examine current operating reality in relation to the Success in Practice narrative.

Section learning outcomes
  • Distinguish the current practices that reinforce strategic progress from those that constrain it.
  • Distinguish results that evidence progress from the activities that produce them.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Section 1 · Awareness

Section intent: This section establishes the full conceptual architecture of Unit 3 — the KISS framework, the OKR anatomy, and the three-step path from KISS output to OKR. Participants need to understand all three before they can do the work. Do not rush this section — the quality of the Integration and Application sessions depends entirely on how well these foundations are laid.

Transition from Unit 2: Open by connecting to what was built in Unit 2. Ask — "In Unit 2, we built our Strategic Intent and Success in Practice narrative. Today we answer the harder question: what must actually change in this organisation to make that future real?" This framing positions KISS as a practical act of honesty.

Suggested section time: 30–40 minutes across all five sections.

Facilitator Opening

Frame this unit as the bridge between the SiP the team has built and the execution reality they must now create. The KISS reflection is a direct examination of the organisation against the specific future they have described.

1.1 — Translating Success in Practice into Action
Foundation
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Section 1

Directing participants: Read the central quote aloud — "If the Success in Practice we described is the future organisation, what must change in the organisation today to make that future possible?" Ask participants to sit with it for a moment. Then ask: "What is the one thing that most needs to change in your area to close that gap?" Take 3–4 responses. Do not evaluate — just note them. They will resurface in the KISS exercise.

Framing the purpose: Emphasise that KISS is a targeted filter applied against the specific future the team has described. Generality is the enemy of this exercise — push for the operating reality.

Suggested time: 5–7 minutes.

, the leadership team has produced a Strategy Intent statement and Success in Practice (SiP) narrative. These describe what, why, how, and when the organisation will deliver its value to stakeholders — and what success looks like in practice.

They provide a vivid and shared description of the future organisation: how customers experience the enterprise, how operations function, how people collaborate, and what value the organisation consistently creates.

The question now becomes: If the Success in Practice we described is the future organisation, what must change in the organisation today to make that future possible?

The next step is to move from this future description to a practical execution agenda. However, before defining Objectives and Key Results, the organisation must first examine its current operating reality in relation to the SiP statement.

1.2 — KISS: Keep · Improve · Start · Stop
4 Reflective Filters
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Section 2

Directing through the four sub-accordions: Expand KEEP, IMPROVE, START, and STOP one at a time. For each, ask participants to give a live example from their own function — anchored to the SiP. This prevents the exercise from becoming abstract.

The specificity standard: Draw attention to the existing Facilitator Move note in this section — the difference between "improve communication" and "improve cross-functional handover time from 72 to 24 hours." Reinforce this standard before the Application section exercise begins.

STOP — the hardest filter: Give this extra time. Ask — "What is one thing your function is currently doing that actively contradicts the future we described? What would it take to stop it?" Silence after this question is productive. Let it run.

Suggested time: 8–10 minutes.

The Strategy2Results® process introduces a reflective step designed to answer this question. The SiP statement is passed through a simple but powerful filter called KISS — Keep, Improve, Start, Stop.

Each element asks leaders to examine the organisation in relation to the future state described in the SiP narrative:

◆ KEEP — Protect what is already working▾

Identifies the practices, capabilities, and behaviours that are already moving the organisation toward the SiP and should therefore be protected and reinforced. These are strategic assets that must not be disrupted in the pursuit of change.

◆ IMPROVE — Strengthen what is partially working▾

Highlights areas where the organisation is already moving in the right direction but where performance, coordination, or capability must strengthen to support the SiP more fully. These are often the highest-leverage interventions.

◆ START — Introduce what does not yet exist▾

Identifies new practices, capabilities, or initiatives that do not yet exist but will be necessary for the organisation to operate in the way described in the SiP. These represent genuine strategic gaps.

◆ STOP — Eliminate what contradicts the SiP▾

Surfaces activities, routines, or commitments that consume energy and resources but do not contribute to the future organisation described in the SiP. Stopping is often harder than starting — and more strategically valuable.

Critical principle: Every KISS reflection must be anchored to the Success in Practice statement. Leaders evaluate the organisation against the future organisation they have collectively described.

Facilitator Move

When leaders generate KISS items, insist on specificity. "Improve cross-functional handover time from 72 hours to 24 hours" is a KISS reflection. Push for the operating reality.

1.4 — OKR Anatomy: Objectives & Key Results
Structure & Formula
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Section 4

Directing through the OKR anatomy: Walk through the two panels (Objectives and Key Results) side by side. Ask participants: "Think of a goal your team set in the last 12 months. Was it an Objective, a Key Result, or a task disguised as one of these?"

The Key Result Construction Guide: Expand the sub-accordion and walk through all four elements with participants. Then give a live example — ask a participant to state a goal they are working toward, and workshop it through the formula in real time. One concrete example does more than ten theoretical ones.

The critical distinction to land: Draw specific attention to the red box — outcomes versus activities. Ask: "Can you check it off a task list? If yes — it's a task." This single test is the most useful tool in the room. Reinforce it every time a task-disguised-as-KR appears in the session.

Suggested time: 8–10 minutes.

Objective Key Results (OKRs) translate the identified changes into measurable progress.

Objectives

Define strategic ambition. Must be: Qualitative · Ambitious · Action-Oriented · Inspirational · Memorable. If you cannot wear it on a T-shirt, it is not worth pursuing.

Key Results

Define measurable progress. Formula: Verb of Change + Metric + From X to Y + By Deadline. Example: Increase digital transaction completion rate from 40% to 75% by Q4 2026.

Key Result Construction Guide — 4 Elements▾
Verb of Change

Indicates the direction of improvement. E.g.: Increase, Reduce, Achieve, Improve, Maintain.

Metric

A clear and measurable indicator. E.g.: Customer satisfaction score, cycle time, adoption rate.

From X to Y

Defines the baseline and target. E.g.: From 40% to 75%. Always establish a starting point.

Deadline

Specifies when the result must be achieved. E.g.: By Q4 2026, By end of FY.

Critical Distinction: OKRs define outcomes. If a statement can be checked off a task list — "launch new platform" or "run customer survey" — it is a task. Those are activities that support the Key Result. Key Results describe the impact those activities must produce.

1.5 — Three Steps: From KISS Output to OKRs
3 Steps

Facilitator Move

After each Objective is proposed, ask the room: "Is this statement something we could announce publicly with pride?" If the answer is no — it lacks ambition or clarity. Objectives that describe activities ("Implement new CRM") are tasks disguised as goals.

Section 2 · Intelligence — Why

Why the KISS-to-OKR Sequence Matters

Ignition Point · Most OKR failures are reflection failures. The KISS-OKR transition provides the missing bridge between strategic intent and measurable execution.

Section learning outcomes

  • Recognise the cost of setting targets before the path to the future state is understood.

, but that enthusiasm often sours into frustration. Teams end up with vague goals and Key Results that feel like just another to-do list. Meetings shift from strategic conversations to tedious reporting sessions where everyone is going through the motions.

The usual cause: organisations try to measure progress before they have had an honest conversation about where they are actually standing.

When leadership moves straight from a big vision to hard targets, they unintentionally skip the reality check. This creates predictable human problems:

Disconnected Targets

People are asked to hit numbers without anyone acknowledging the hurdles that make those numbers nearly impossible.

Fragmented Efforts

The Activity Trap

People default to measuring busyness because they are not sure how to prove they are achieving real outcomes.

Essentially, the organisation starts measuring the finish line before it has mapped the terrain.

2.2 — The KISS Reflection as the Missing Bridge
4 Honest Realities
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Section 2.2

The KISS reflection is where the team pauses to be honest. It forces leaders to look at the Success in Practice vision they have created and ask: "What is actually happening on the ground right now?"

This reflection creates a bridge between strategic imagination and operational reality. The team builds OKRs from evidence. The KISS discussion brings real issues to the surface:

  • The strengths to protect and lean into.
  • The friction points that are quietly draining energy.
  • The gaps where genuine support or investment is needed.
  • The old habits to finally let go of because they no longer serve the strategy.

Panoramic Enterprise OKR Prioritisation

Once initial OKRs have been drafted, the leadership team determines which Objectives deserve enterprise focus now. Strategy execution fails when too many priorities are pursued simultaneously.

Each team member reviews proposed Objectives and votes on which represent the most critical strategic movements at this moment.

The Less is More Rule: No more than 4 Enterprise Objectives. No more than 3 Key Results per Objective. This constraint forces explicit trade-offs, ensuring execution energy is concentrated.

The Prioritisation Matrix: Impact versus Effort

The matrix evaluates initiatives along two dimensions: Impact — the degree of strategic value delivered if achieved — and Effort — the time, resources, coordination, and organisational change required.

The leadership team must pay particular attention to initiatives in Strategic Catalysts, Accelerated Enablers, and Core Strategic Drivers — these represent the highest leverage execution signals.

Section 5 · Application — In Practice

Working Session: KISS Mapping, OKR Construction & Prioritisation

Three Tasks · Group KISS reflection · Enterprise OKRs · Prioritisation Matrix

Section learning outcomes
  • Formulate clear and measurable outcomes for each enterprise priority.
  • Establish the link between each outcome, its contributing roles and the evidence of progress.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Section 5 · Application

Section intent: This is the production section — the session where the concepts become outputs. Three tasks run in sequence. Protect the time for each and do not allow them to overlap. The quality of the outputs depends entirely on the discipline of the sequence.

Session architecture:

  • Worked Example — reference case (5 min, facilitator-led): show participants what a complete KISS-to-OKR sequence looks like before they attempt their own
  • Task 1 — Group KISS Reflection (15–20 min): group captures KISS items across all four domains
  • Task 2 — OKR Builder (20–25 min): individual drafting followed by de-labelled group synthesis
  • Task 3 — Prioritisation Matrix (10–15 min): collective placement and debate

Non-negotiable facilitation principles: Individual drafting must precede group discussion in Tasks 1 and 2. Written submissions prevent the anchoring bias where vocal leaders dominate the output before quieter voices have formed their perspective.

Suggested total section time: 55–70 minutes.

Facilitator Instructions

This is the active working session. Complete each task in sequence with the full leadership team. In Task 1 participants record their KISS reflection across the four domains. In Task 2 they draft Key Results and the group de-labels the OKR landscape. Task 3 applies the Prioritisation Matrix to concentrate execution energy.

Facilitator · Worked Example: KISS Reflection & Strategic Themes
Reference Case
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Worked Example

Purpose: Show the group what a complete KISS-to-OKR sequence looks like before they attempt their own. This prevents blank-page paralysis and establishes the quality standard for specificity.

Directing participants: Walk through the example KISS table and the resulting Objectives. For each, ask — "Notice how the KISS item maps directly to the Objective. There is no gap. The Objective is the direct consequence of what the KISS reflection surfaced."

Quality check: After walking through the example, ask participants — "At this level of specificity, could you trace every OKR back to a specific KISS observation? That is the standard we are working toward."

Suggested time: 5 minutes, facilitator-led.

◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Task 1

Running the task: Before the group begins, remind participants — "Every item you enter must be anchored to the SiP. For example: 'we should improve cross-functional handover time from 72 to 24 hours because the SiP describes seamless execution rhythm.'"

Individual before collective: Ask participants to write their own inputs first (2–3 minutes per domain), then share with the group. The facilitator captures the agreed items in the tool. This prevents the first speaker from dominating the KISS landscape.

After all four domains are captured: Ask the group — "Look across all four domains. What are the 2–3 patterns that keep appearing? What does the organisation most consistently need to START, STOP, or IMPROVE relative to the SiP?" These patterns become the strategic themes that anchor the Objectives in Task 2.

Time discipline: Aim for 3–4 items per KISS element per domain. More than 5 items per element means the group has not prioritised — push for selection.

5.2 — Task 2 · Determine Key Results & De-label the OKR Landscape
4 Objectives · 2 Key Results Each
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Task 2

Running the OKR build: Work through each Objective one at a time with the full group. For each, allow 3–4 minutes for individual Key Result drafting before the group discusses. Apply the formula rigorously — Verb of Change + Metric + From X to Y + Deadline. If a Key Result cannot pass the "can you check it off a task list?" test, it is a task.

The alignment test in real time: Before a KR is entered, ask — "Does this result move the whole enterprise forward — or only one function?" This is Step 4 from the Integration section applied live. Do not skip it for any KR.

Assembling the de-labelled landscape: Once all KRs are entered, click "Assemble Landscape." Walk the group through the de-labelled view and ask — "Looking at this landscape without knowing who proposed what — which KRs are clearly enterprise-level? Which ones feel functional even without the label? What does that tell you?"

The Less is More Rule: Enforce it here — maximum 4 Objectives, maximum 3 Key Results per Objective. Any KR that does not survive the de-labelled scrutiny should be released.

Suggested time: 20–25 minutes.

Facilitator · Task 3 · Collective Intelligence: Prioritisation Matrix
Impact × Effort · Assign OKRs
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Task 3

Before placing any items: Establish shared definitions — ask the group: "What does 'high impact' mean for this organisation specifically? What does 'high effort' mean in our context — time, money, change management, or all three?" Without shared definitions, the placements will be meaningless.

Running the exercise: Place OKRs one at a time. For each, ask the group to call out their placement before showing any individual view. Disagreements are the data — do not resolve them too quickly. Ask: "Why do you see this as high impact? What would need to be true for this to be low effort?"

The existing Facilitator Move note applies here: Listen for disagreements about impact versus effort — this is where collective intelligence is built in real time. The quality of the debate is as valuable as the final matrix.

Closing the session: After the matrix is complete, ask — "Looking at this matrix — what does it tell you about where to focus your first 90 days? What must start immediately? What can wait?" Then direct the group to produce their individual portfolio commitments in the participant file before closing.

Suggested time: 10–15 minutes.

Evaluate each OKR initiative against Impact and Effort. The matrix becomes the strategic conversation device for aligning the leadership team on execution priority.

As the team debates placements, listen for disagreements about what constitutes "high impact" versus "high effort." These disagreements are where collective intelligence is built. Do not rush to consensus — the quality of the debate is as valuable as the final placement.

Unit Summary
Unit 3 synthesis
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Unit Summary

Closing the unit: Read the summary aloud or ask a participant to read it. It is intentionally concise — it crystallises the arc of the unit without adding new content. Let it do its work without embellishment.

Return to the opening question: Go back to the question you asked at the start of Awareness — "If the Success in Practice we described is the future organisation, what must change today to make that future possible?" Ask 2–3 participants: "Looking at the OKRs we have just built — have we answered that question? Where are the remaining gaps?"

Tangible outputs check: Before closing, confirm the following have been produced:

  • A completed group KISS reflection across all four domains (Task 1)
  • Enterprise OKRs — maximum 4 Objectives, maximum 3 KRs each — reviewed in de-labelled form (Task 2)
  • A completed Prioritisation Matrix with collective agreement on execution priority (Task 3)
  • A Session Report exported for distribution (Export)

Closing commitment: Ask each participant to complete this sentence privately — "The one thing I am committing to before our next session to make these OKRs real is…" Invite 2–3 to share. These are the accountability anchors between now and Unit 4.

Transition to Unit 4: Close with — "We have built our Strategic Intent, described our future state through SiP, and now defined how we will measure our progress through OKRs. Unit 4 examines the competitive environment — the external forces that will shape, enable, or resist the journey toward the organisation we have described."
