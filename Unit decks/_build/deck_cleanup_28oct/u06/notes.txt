2.3 — PERFORMANCE SCORECARD DEVELOPMENT: THE THREE CATEGORIES

HOW TO TEACH IT:

1. Link to the execution bridges.
Say: "The execution bridges carry enterprise results to the people who deliver them. The performance scorecard is where that connection reaches the individual: it states the measures on which a role's performance is assessed and the weight each carries."

2. Name the three categories.
Say: "A well-designed scorecard tracks performance in three categories: Strategy, Operational and Behavioural. Each gives a different angle on whether the organisation is progressing towards its strategic intent."

3. Teach Strategy.
Say: "Strategy measures are the strategic OKRs directly connected to the organisation's multi-year strategy. They ask: are we making progress on what matters most for where we are going? They typically carry 30–50% of the scorecard at senior levels, and less for frontline roles. They are reviewed quarterly, with executive visibility."

4. Teach Operational.
Say: "Operational measures are the departmental KPIs that track the operational engine: efficiency, output, quality and service delivery. They ask: are operations performing at the standard required to sustain the strategy? They carry 30–50% across most role levels, highest for frontline and specialist roles. They are reviewed monthly or fortnightly."

5. Teach Behavioural.
Say: "Behavioural measures cover how individuals demonstrate the organisation's values and leadership behaviours, assessed through 360° feedback and peer observation. They ask: how are our results being achieved? They carry 20–40% across role levels, higher for leadership and customer-facing roles. They are reviewed bi-annually, through structured 360° feedback."
Point to the bar on the slide. Say: "How results are achieved matters as much as what results are achieved."

6. Ask, now that the three categories are taught.
Ask: "If your CEO's scorecard were published tomorrow, what share of it would be Strategy, Operational and Behavioural, and does that reflect the role?"
Take two or three answers.

7. Bridge to 2.3.1.
Say: "Next, how a scorecard is put together for a role."

2.3.1 — DEVELOPING A PERFORMANCE SCORECARD

HOW TO TEACH IT:

1. Start from the enterprise Key Results.
Say: "The Strategy measures come from the Key Results the role contributes to, as set out in the S2R® Contribution Map™."

2. Add the Operational KPIs.
Say: "Name the KPIs that show whether the role's function performs at the standard the strategy requires."

3. Add the Behavioural measures.
Say: "Name the values and leadership behaviours expected of the role, and how they will be assessed."

4. Weight the three categories.
Say: "Set the weight of each category to reflect the role's level and its influence on the strategy. The weights add up to 100%."

5. Make it transparent.
Say: "The role holder understands the KPIs, targets, weightings, behavioural standards and rating criteria on which performance will be assessed."

6. Show how the weighting changes with the role's level.
Say: "At executive level, the leader is measured on the cumulative outcome that the team's activities produce. The scorecard of a CEO therefore carries a higher Strategy weight than the scorecard of a frontline role. In Section 5, Activity 3, your group designs one scorecard, for its CEO."

7. Close Section 2 and bridge to Section 3.
Say: "Performance management bridges strategy and execution when it builds commitment to results, and the scorecard carries that commitment to each role. Section 3 examines where performance management becomes vulnerable across executive perspectives, and how its practice is evolving."

5.3 — ACTIVITY 3: DESIGN THE CEO PERFORMANCE SCORECARD (CAPSTONE WORK)

HOW TO TEACH IT:

1. Open from part 2.3.
Say: "In part 2.3 we set out the three categories of a performance scorecard and their typical weighting: Strategy 30–50% at senior levels, Operational 30–50% and Behavioural 20–40%. At executive level, the leader is measured on the cumulative outcome that the team's activities produce."

2. Brief the activity.
Say: "Your group designs one scorecard, for your CEO. The Strategy measures start from the enterprise Key Result you chose in Activity 1, and you can add a second Key Result from your Unit 3 OKRs. Then name the Operational KPIs and the Behavioural measures, and weight the three categories so that the weights add up to 100%."

3. Walk through the template.
Say: "The template on your page shows the enterprise Key Result from Activity 1. You complete five fields."
Strategy · Key Result 1: what the CEO is measured on for the enterprise Key Result from Activity 1, and its weight.
Strategy · Key Result 2 (optional): what the CEO is measured on for a second Key Result from your Unit 3 OKRs, and its weight.
Operational: the operational KPIs the CEO is measured on, with their targets, and their weight.
Behavioural: the values and leadership behaviours the CEO is measured on, how they are assessed, and their weight.
Total weight: the weights add up to 100%.

4. Ask the weighting question.
Ask: "Does the Strategy weight reflect that the CEO is measured on the cumulative outcome the team's activities produce?"

5. Read the completion check.
Say: "Before you confirm, check four things."
1. The Strategy measures come from your enterprise Key Results.
2. Each category has a weight, and the weights add up to 100%.
3. The weighting reflects the CEO's level and influence on the strategy.
4. The Behavioural measures state how they are assessed.

6. Bridge to Activity 4.
Say: "With the CEO's measures set, Activity 4 sets the vital check-up for the Key Result itself."

ON THE PORTAL, AFTER THE TEACHING:
Participants work as a group in Activity 3 of Section 5 of the participant file. This is Capstone work. The group chooses its scribe for this work. The scribe types what the group agrees, and the entries appear on every member's page.
1. The group sets the Strategy measures and their weight.
2. The group sets the Operational and Behavioural measures and their weights.
3. The group checks that the weights add up to 100%, then goes to Activity 4.
