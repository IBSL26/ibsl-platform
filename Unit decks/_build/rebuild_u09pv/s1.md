## Understanding Where Performance Becomes Constrained

Strategy depends on the organisation’s ability to convert its strategic intent into coordinated contributions and measurable results. This requires two connected dimensions of performance:

**Organisational performance.** The enterprise’s ability to create, deliver and sustain value. It reflects how effectively structures, processes, resources, capabilities, governance and operating systems work together to produce strategic outcomes.

**People performance.** How effectively individuals and teams understand expectations, make decisions, exercise judgement, apply capabilities, collaborate and deliver their contributions to organisational results.

Both dimensions operate within the same enterprise system. When organisational conditions are weak, even capable and committed people may struggle to perform. They experience delays, conflicting priorities, unclear responsibilities, resource shortages and barriers beyond their authority to resolve.

When people performance is weak, the organisation may have adequate resources and well-designed processes but still experience poor judgement, inconsistent behaviours, weak collaboration, limited ownership and unreliable delivery.

These conditions often reinforce one another. For example, unclear decision rights create hesitation among managers. Repeated hesitation then encourages senior leaders to centralise decisions further, increasing the original bottleneck.

The 7-Clog Diagnostic Map identifies seven recurring conditions that constrain the organisation’s capacity to deliver its strategy. The diagnostic examines both the condition of the organisational system and the performance patterns emerging within it.

## The Seven Performance Clogs

Select each clog to examine its organisational conditions, effects on people, observable patterns, early warnings and strategic consequences.

### Clog 01 — Structural Misfit

ABCV element: Boundaries

**Organisational performance — System reality.** The organisational design no longer supports the work required by the strategy. Roles, reporting relationships, governance arrangements, accountability boundaries and functional interfaces may still reflect earlier business priorities. Work requiring integrated contributions is divided across departments without sufficient coordination or shared ownership.

**People performance — Contribution reality.** Employees may understand their individual responsibilities but struggle to see how their contributions connect to the enterprise outcome. Managers can become protective of functional territory, while teams experience competing instructions and repeated handoffs. Performance suffers when people are held accountable for outcomes they cannot deliver without decisions or inputs controlled elsewhere.

Observable performance patterns:

-   Duplication of work across functions

-   Repeated bottlenecks at functional handoffs

-   Fragmented ownership of strategic outcomes

-   Front-office and back-office coordination failures

-   Conflicting priorities between departments

Early warning signals:

-   Increasing time spent coordinating routine work

-   Greater dependence on informal relationships and workarounds

-   Repeated escalation of cross-functional disagreements

-   Performance gaps concentrated at handoff points

**Strategic impact.** Resources and effort are absorbed by internal friction. Individuals may achieve their functional targets while enterprise outcomes remain weak. The organisation loses the ability to coordinate delivery across its full value chain.

**Diagnostic question.** Are results being constrained by how responsibilities, authority and work are organised?

**Worked example.** A customer service team is measured on response time, but it depends on operations and finance to resolve enquiries. No function owns the complete customer-resolution outcome. Staff work hard, yet customer complaints continue.

### Clog 02 — Capability Gaps

ABCV element: Arena

**Organisational performance — System reality.** The organisation lacks, or has uneven access to, the capabilities required to succeed in its chosen strategic arena. Gaps may involve technical expertise, management capacity, specialised equipment, operating processes, digital competence, partnerships, funding or the ability to scale. Existing capabilities may support current operations but remain insufficient for the new strategic ambition.

**People performance — Contribution reality.** Individuals may lack the skills, experience, knowledge, tools or support needed to meet new performance expectations. Others may possess strong capabilities but be deployed in roles that underuse them. Repeated reliance on a few specialists creates overload and makes delivery vulnerable. Without adequate development and support, employees can lose confidence, improvise unsuitable methods or avoid unfamiliar tasks.

Observable performance patterns:

-   Strategic initiatives stall despite clear objectives

-   Repeated rework and inconsistent quality

-   Critical work depends on a small number of specialists

-   Similar teams produce widely different performance results

-   Outsourcing becomes necessary for strategically essential work

Early warning signals:

-   Training needs persist across several review cycles

-   Key employees carry unsustainable workloads

-   New initiatives repeatedly miss readiness milestones

-   Capability gaps emerge when operations expand

**Strategic impact.** The enterprise cannot consistently deliver the value promised by its strategic positioning. People face expectations that exceed available capability, and the organisation struggles to compete, scale or sustain improved results.

**Diagnostic question.** Does the organisation possess and deploy the capabilities necessary to operate successfully in its chosen arena?

**Worked example.** A healthcare provider introduces a digital patient-care model, but staff lack the necessary digital skills and the organisation has insufficient technical support. Service quality varies, adoption slows and employees become frustrated.

### Clog 03 — Decision Bottlenecks

ABCV element: Boundaries

**Organisational performance — System reality.** Decision rights, delegation limits, approval processes or governance layers prevent decisions from being made at the appropriate level and speed. Authority is concentrated away from the people closest to the relevant information or delivery responsibility. Escalation processes do not consistently distinguish strategic decisions from routine operational decisions.

**People performance — Contribution reality.** Managers become hesitant to exercise judgement because they are uncertain about their authority or fear being blamed for decisions. Teams learn to seek approval for matters they could reasonably resolve. Senior leaders become overloaded, while middle managers lose initiative and confidence. Accountability becomes difficult to enforce where responsibility is assigned without corresponding authority.

Observable performance patterns:

-   Routine matters repeatedly require senior approval

-   Decisions return to meetings without resolution

-   Project teams wait for sign-off before proceeding

-   Managers avoid taking ownership of reasonable decisions

-   Senior executives become involved in minor operational matters

Early warning signals:

-   Increasing decision turnaround times

-   Growing queues of unresolved approvals

-   Recurring agenda items without closure

-   Teams repeatedly report that they are waiting for a decision

**Strategic impact.** Execution velocity slows because work cannot progress at the speed required. Employees lose ownership, management capacity is consumed by avoidable escalation, and the organisation becomes less responsive to customers and environmental changes.

**Diagnostic question.** Are decisions being made by the right people, with sufficient authority, information and speed?

**Worked example.** A branch manager identifies a service problem but cannot approve a small process adjustment without head-office authorisation. The delay affects customers, although the branch has the knowledge and resources to respond.

### Clog 04 — Misaligned Metrics & Incentives

ABCV element: Value Proposition

**Organisational performance — System reality.** The performance management system measures, rewards or reinforces activities that do not adequately reflect the strategic value the organisation intends to deliver. Functional scorecards may remain unchanged after strategic priorities shift. Individual and departmental targets can encourage local optimisation while enterprise-wide outcomes deteriorate.

**People performance — Contribution reality.** Employees naturally pay attention to the outcomes for which they are evaluated, recognised or rewarded. When metrics are poorly aligned, people may prioritise target achievement over customer value, collaboration, quality or sustainable results. Even committed employees face tension when doing what is strategically necessary threatens their formal performance rating.

Observable performance patterns:

-   Strong departmental KPI scores coexist with disappointing enterprise results

-   Teams protect their own targets at the expense of shared outcomes

-   Employees focus on measurable volume rather than quality or value

-   Performance discussions concentrate on score achievement without examining contribution

-   Incentives discourage collaboration or long-term improvement

Early warning signals:

-   Reported performance improves while customer outcomes deteriorate

-   Departments present contradictory accounts of success

-   Targets are achieved through reclassification or selective reporting

-   Employees question why strategically valuable work receives little recognition

**Strategic impact.** The enterprise generates measurable activity without achieving the intended value proposition. Performance systems unintentionally reinforce behaviours that undermine the strategy, reducing both organisational effectiveness and the integrity of individual accountability.

**Diagnostic question.** Are people being measured and rewarded for contributions that genuinely produce the intended strategic value?

**Worked example.** A customer contact centre rewards agents for the number of calls completed. Agents shorten conversations to meet targets, but repeat calls increase and customer satisfaction falls.

### Clog 05 — Cultural Resistance

ABCV element: Value Proposition

**Organisational performance — System reality.** Established norms, shared assumptions, informal rules and everyday practices do not support the behavioural changes required by the strategy. The formal strategy may demand collaboration, experimentation, customer responsiveness or accountability, while the prevailing culture reinforces hierarchy, risk avoidance, silence or functional protection.

**People performance — Contribution reality.** Individuals interpret the strategy through previous experiences and the behaviours they observe being rewarded or tolerated. Employees may understand the new direction but hesitate to adopt it because they do not trust its sustainability, feel unsafe challenging established practices or see leaders behaving inconsistently. Resistance can also signal unresolved concerns about feasibility, fairness or capability.

Observable performance patterns:

-   Teams return to familiar practices under pressure

-   Employees comply formally without changing everyday behaviours

-   Constructive challenge and initiative remain limited

-   Cross-functional collaboration is resisted

-   Informal workplace narratives undermine formal strategic messages

Early warning signals:

-   Employees remain silent during important performance discussions

-   Low participation in improvement initiatives

-   Repeated expressions of scepticism about change

-   Leaders publicly endorse behaviours they do not consistently demonstrate

**Strategic impact.** The organisation struggles to translate strategic intent into sustained behaviour. New systems and processes are adopted superficially, while underlying habits continue to shape delivery. Improvements are difficult to sustain because the operating culture repeatedly pulls performance back toward established practices.

**Diagnostic question.** Which established beliefs, norms and behaviours are reinforcing or obstructing the contributions required by the strategy?

**Worked example.** A company introduces a customer-first strategy, but employees are still criticised for taking initiative outside rigid procedures. Teams continue escalating customer problems rather than resolving them.

### Clog 06 — Technology / Infrastructure Lag

ABCV element: Competition

**Organisational performance — System reality.** Technology, information architecture, operational infrastructure or physical assets are not sufficiently reliable, integrated or scalable to support strategic demands. Legacy platforms create fragmented information flows, weak visibility, manual intervention and constraints on service capacity. The infrastructure may also restrict the speed at which the organisation adapts to external developments.

**People performance — Contribution reality.** Employees spend excessive time correcting data, reconciling records, duplicating entries and finding workarounds. Managers make decisions with incomplete or outdated information. Employees may be evaluated against efficiency standards that existing systems cannot support. Repeated technology frustrations also reduce motivation and confidence in improvement initiatives.

Observable performance patterns:

-   Heavy reliance on spreadsheets and manual reporting

-   Duplicate data entry across systems

-   Delayed or inconsistent management information

-   Operational errors increase with workload

-   Employees maintain unofficial systems to complete essential tasks

Early warning signals:

-   Reporting cycles become progressively longer

-   Discrepancies appear between information sources

-   Routine tasks demand increasing manual effort

-   System downtime or integration failures affect critical delivery

**Strategic impact.** The organisation loses operational speed, information quality and the ability to scale reliably. People devote capacity to compensating for infrastructure limitations, reducing time available for value-creating work. Competitive responsiveness weakens as the enterprise struggles to match required standards of efficiency, reliability or service.

**Diagnostic question.** Can the organisation’s technology and infrastructure support the speed, reliability, visibility and scale required by its strategy?

**Worked example.** A logistics company promises real-time delivery visibility, but its transport and customer systems do not communicate. Staff compile updates manually, customers receive inconsistent information and service confidence declines.

### Clog 07 — Leadership Avoidance

ABCV element: Arena + Boundaries

**Organisational performance — System reality.** Leadership mechanisms fail to resolve material trade-offs, repeated underperformance, resource conflicts, accountability gaps or strategic tensions. Difficult matters are discussed without binding decisions or clear follow-through. Governance and review forums become reporting rituals rather than mechanisms for resolving the conditions obstructing performance.

**People performance — Contribution reality.** Managers and employees receive mixed signals about priorities, standards and consequences. Some learn that difficult issues can be postponed without consequence. Others become discouraged when their concerns repeatedly go unresolved. Confidence in leadership declines, and individuals may protect themselves by avoiding ownership, escalating responsibility or lowering their performance expectations.

Observable performance patterns:

-   Strategic problems recur across successive reviews

-   Decisions are recorded but not consistently implemented

-   Underperformance continues without meaningful intervention

-   Leaders repeatedly change priorities without resolving trade-offs

-   Accountability is assigned without the support required to deliver

Early warning signals:

-   The same issues appear in multiple performance cycles

-   Employees stop raising problems because they expect no action

-   Escalations remain unresolved despite leadership attention

-   Teams become uncertain about which priorities matter most

**Strategic impact.** Strategic focus weakens as unresolved issues accumulate. The organisation loses direction and delivery discipline, while people become less willing to take ownership. Over time, leadership avoidance can reinforce all six other performance clogs because their underlying causes remain unaddressed.

**Diagnostic question.** Are leaders making and following through on the decisions necessary to remove performance constraints?

**Worked example.** A management team repeatedly identifies poor coordination between sales and operations but avoids changing responsibilities because two senior executives disagree. The problem continues, and both teams are blamed for missed delivery targets.

## 1.3.1 — The ABCV Diagnostic Connection

The ABCV framework helps locate the strategic dimension being distorted by a performance clog.

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **ABCV element**        **Strategic concern**                                                                                                    **Related clogs**
  ----------------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ ---------------------------------------------------------------
  Arena                   Does the organisation have the capability and focus to operate successfully in its chosen strategic space?               Capability Gaps; Leadership Avoidance

  Boundaries              Do organisational arrangements, decision rights and governance enable disciplined, coordinated delivery?                 Structural Misfit; Decision Bottlenecks; Leadership Avoidance

  Competition             Can the enterprise respond and perform at the speed, reliability and standard required in its competitive environment?   Technology / Infrastructure Lag

  Value Proposition       Do organisational behaviours, measures and contributions produce the value promised to stakeholders?                     Misaligned Metrics & Incentives; Cultural Resistance
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These are primary diagnostic associations. A clog may affect several ABCV elements simultaneously. For example, inadequate technology may primarily affect competitive responsiveness but also prevent the organisation from delivering its value proposition. Similarly, a structural misfit may originate in organisational boundaries while undermining the chosen strategic arena.

ABCV helps identify where strategic intent is being distorted; the 7-Clog Diagnostic Map helps explain the conditions producing that distortion.

## 1.3.2 — The Three Performance Lines of Sight from the Seven Clogs

The central discipline is to distinguish three levels of performance evidence. Each line of sight reveals a different part of the performance problem and helps leaders move from symptoms to causes.

**1. Performance outcome.** What result is falling short? Establish the intended strategic outcome, the actual result and the size or nature of the gap.

**2. Performance behaviour.** What are people or teams doing, or unable to do, that contributes to the gap? Observe specific decisions, actions, omissions, collaboration patterns and delivery practices.

**3. Performance condition.** What organisational arrangements, capabilities, incentives or leadership practices are causing or reinforcing that behaviour? Test the seven clogs to explain why the pattern persists.

This distinction helps leaders avoid treating every disappointing result as an individual performance failure. It also keeps people accountable for actions within their control while recognising the organisation’s responsibility for enabling conditions.

A single performance outcome can reflect several interacting clogs. The following example traces the three lines of sight before identifying the organisational conditions that require attention.

## 1.3.3 — Worked Diagnostic Example: Improving Customer Service

**Enterprise Objective.** Deliver a reliable and responsive customer service experience.

**Enterprise Key Result.** Increase customer enquiries resolved within 48 hours from 65% to 90% by year-end.

Performance figures are illustrative.

### Line of Sight 1 — Performance Outcome

Only 65% of customer enquiries are resolved within 48 hours against a target of 90%. The enterprise is falling short of its intended customer responsiveness and service reliability.

### Line of Sight 2 — Performance Behaviour

Service agents process enquiries and escalate cases requiring other departments. Cases are frequently transferred without end-to-end ownership, managers wait for approval on routine refunds, and staff spend time requesting updates rather than resolving customer problems. Employees may be achieving their activity targets while the collective resolution outcome remains weak.

### Line of Sight 3 — Performance Condition

The diagnostic identifies the organisational conditions that help explain the behaviour:

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Evidence**                                          **Clog**                          **How it constrains performance**
  ----------------------------------------------------- --------------------------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  40% of delayed cases require another department.      Structural Misfit                 Fragmented ownership and weak cross-functional handoffs delay resolution.

  Routine refunds require senior-management approval.   Decision Bottlenecks              People closest to the enquiry lack sufficient authority to resolve it.

  Employee scorecards emphasise enquiries processed.    Misaligned Metrics & Incentives   Volume is reinforced more strongly than complete and timely resolution.

  Employees cannot track cases after escalation.        Technology / Infrastructure Lag   Fragmented information prevents timely follow-through.
  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Organisational performance interpretation.** The coordination processes, decision rights, measures and information systems are not sufficiently aligned to the customer-resolution outcome. These conditions create recurring delays across the service chain.

**People performance interpretation.** Customer service employees may complete assigned tasks but still lack the authority, information and cross-functional support needed to resolve cases. Any individual capability or behavioural gaps must be assessed against these conditions and the standards within each person’s control.

**Strategic interpretation.** The customer value proposition promises responsive service, but operating arrangements do not consistently enable its delivery. The principal ABCV distortion concerns the Value Proposition, with contributing constraints in Boundaries and Competition.

**Required management response.** Clarify end-to-end resolution ownership, adjust routine decision authority, align measures to resolution outcomes and improve case visibility across departments. Provide coaching or accountability interventions where evidence confirms individual capability or behavioural gaps.

## Closing Diagnostic Principle

Performance is produced through people operating within an organisational system. A sound diagnosis examines the result, the behaviours shaping contributions and the organisational conditions that enable or constrain them.

The 7-Clog Diagnostic Map helps leaders identify where these relationships have become misaligned, which ABCV elements are affected and what must change to restore performance.
