Module 4 · Grounding · Unit 12

Sustaining Performance

Grounding · Pillar 3 of DIG

Grounding establishes the organisational capabilities and healthy operating status required to anchor and sustain strategic performance. It considers how the enterprise is configured to deliver results, the organisational health that sustains performance, and how both are leveraged over time.

The Grounding learning journey progresses from Unit 10 — ESRG Alignment (organisational operating environment), to Unit 11 — Culture Reinforcement & Organisational Health (culture), and then to this unit, which leverages both to sustain high performance.

Module learning outcomes Configure organisational operating conditions to support strategic priorities and enable consistent enterprise performance. Shape organisational culture by establishing and embedding the conditions and practices that strengthen organisational health. Integrate organisational capabilities and cultural practices into a coherent operating environment that supports sustained high performance. Institutionalise organisational performance sustainability through mechanisms that preserve strategic alignment, strengthen adaptability and maintain performance continuity.
Key learning outcomes Distinguish short-term performance attainment from sustained institutional capability. Maintain continuous sight of strategic, operational and behavioural performance across the enterprise’s contributions. Recognise the sources of performance drift and the early erosion of strategic coherence. Exercise forward-looking executive judgement that protects organisational value and long-term performance.

Section 1 · Awareness — What

What Sustains Performance

Performance Gravity, the Three Lines of Sight that show it, and the four KISS decisions that hold performance against it.

Section learning outcomes
  • Recognise the force that pulls achieved results back towards average.
  • Identify the leadership decisions that hold performance against that pull.
1.1 — Performance Gravity

Achieving a result and holding it are two separate leadership tasks. The strategy, the contributions and the performance system built across this programme produce results. Once achieved, those results come under a steady pull back towards the organisation’s historical average. This pull is Performance Gravity.

Performance Gravity works quietly. Results often remain acceptable for several cycles while the conditions and behaviours that produced them weaken underneath. By the time the decline shows in the result, the cost of correcting it is far higher than the cost of noticing it early.

Sustaining performance is the leadership discipline of seeing that pull early and responding to it every cycle.

1.2 — The Three Lines of Sight
Strategic · Operational · Behavioural

Performance Gravity shows first as drift on one of three lines of sight. Sustaining performance means keeping all three in view, every quarter, on the Key Result and the contributions in your S2R® Contribution Map™. Each line is read from a result your leadership team already produces.

Line of sightThe question each quarterThe result it reads
StrategicIs the Key Result still moving towards its Success in Practice?The Key Result against its WARD waypoint (Unit 6)
OperationalAre the organisational conditions still enabling the people’s tasks?The ESRG scan: Operational Readiness, Operational Flow, Delivery Capacity and Decision Responsiveness (Unit 10)
BehaviouralAre people still delivering the agreed behaviours?The Organisational Health Thermometer: the functional health score and the five traits (Unit 11)

Filtering each line through WARD

WARD, set for your Key Result in Unit 6, filters all three lines. Each line has a level it must hold (the waypoint), a point at which it raises a red flag (the alert), a recovery and a Decider.

Drift usually raises an alert on the operational or behavioural line before the Key Result misses its waypoint. A leadership team that reads only the waypoint of the Key Result sees Performance Gravity last.

1.3 — KISS: The Four Sustaining Decisions
Keep · Improve · Stop · Start

At the close of every quarter, the leadership team makes four decisions about the practices, routines, behaviours and conditions behind its results.

K
Keep

Identify, name and protect the practices, routines, behaviours and conditions that are producing reliable results. Effective practices depend on leadership attention; when that attention moves elsewhere, they weaken without notice. Keep makes the implicit explicit: what is producing this result, and what must stay in place for it to continue?

I
Improve

Recognise what was working well and is beginning to work less well, and recalibrate it while the gap is still a signal. Practices that were excellent six months ago may be merely adequate today. Improve corrects them before the gap shows in the result.

S
Stop

Eliminate practices, meetings, processes, reports and approval layers that consume energy without producing commensurate value. Stop is the hardest decision, because every practice has a sponsor and people whose interests it serves.

S
Start

Introduce what the organisation is not yet capable of and will need for the next level of performance. Start invests in future capability while current results make that investment possible.

Section 2 · Intelligence — Why

Why Performance Drifts, and Why KISS Holds It

The mechanism of drift on each line of sight, and the KISS decisions that counter each one.

Section learning outcomes
  • Explain why achieved performance drifts back towards average across strategy, operations and behaviour.
  • Counter drift before it reaches the enterprise result.
2.1 — Why Performance Drifts
Select each line to open it

Performance Gravity works through a different mechanism on each line of sight.

Strategic line · Momentum Illusion

The market, customers and competitors keep moving after the strategy is set. The organisation continues running the approach that produced its results, and the results stay acceptable for a while because momentum carries them forward. The Key Result keeps meeting its waypoints while the Success in Practice it serves moves further away.

How it shows: waypoints met with growing effort; customers referring to alternatives that did not exist when the strategy was set; win rates and renewals moving slightly in the wrong direction.

Operational line · Complexity Creep

Every change in regulation, cost or technology brings a structural response: a new approval, a new report, a new process. Each response made sense when it was created. Together they leave the organisation heavier and slower than its current environment requires, and the conditions named in the Contribution Map begin to constrain the people’s tasks.

How it shows: approvals and processes no one can explain the current purpose of; manual workarounds; budget structures built for conditions that have since changed.

Behavioural line · Execution Regression

Every organisation carries habits, norms and assumptions reinforced over years. Once attention moves away from a new practice, these habits pull behaviour back towards the historical pattern. Review cadences loosen, escalation slows and standards return to earlier levels, often without anyone deciding to relax them.

How it shows: reviews shortened or postponed; issues carried across several cycles; accountability applied unevenly; agreed behaviours visible only when leaders are watching.

Execution Regression weakens the organisation’s ability to respond on the other two lines, because the routines that detect drift are the first to loosen.

2.2 — Why KISS Holds Performance

Each KISS decision answers a specific form of drift.

Line of sightDrift on this lineKISS decisions that counter it
StrategicMomentum IllusionKeep what still earns the result in the current market. Start what the moved market now requires.
OperationalComplexity CreepStop structures built for conditions that no longer exist. Improve conditions that are beginning to constrain delivery.
BehaviouralExecution RegressionKeep the agreed behaviours protected with leadership attention. Improve behaviours that have started to slip.

A leadership team that reads the three lines each quarter and closes each quarter with its KISS decisions holds performance against all three mechanisms.

Section 3 · Extrapolating — Where

Where Drift Shows First, and Where Each KISS Decision Sits

The executive domain where drift surfaces first, the line of sight each role reads first, and the KISS decision each role usually leads.

Section learning outcomes
  • Locate where drift appears first in each executive domain.
  • Assign executive ownership for holding performance in each domain.
3.1 — Where Drift Shows First
Select each role to open it

Drift surfaces first in the executive domain closest to it. Each executive reads one line of sight first and usually leads the KISS decision that answers its drift.

Section 4 · Integration — Collective

The Quarterly Three Lines of Sight

One page each quarter: the three lines of sight read from the worked results, filtered through WARD and closed with KISS.

Section learning outcomes
  • Establish a collective leadership rhythm that sustains performance across every contribution.
  • Coordinate the enterprise response to drift before it reaches the result.
4.1 — The Quarterly Three Lines of Sight Page

Each quarter the leadership team completes one page for each Key Result. The page brings the three lines together, filters each through WARD and closes with the KISS decisions for the next quarter.

Part of the pageWhat the leadership team enters
HeaderKey Result · Quarter · Decider
Each line of sightThe result this quarter · The waypoint · The alert · Alert raised (yes or no) · The recovery and its owner · When the Decider checks it
KISS for next quarterKeep · Improve · Stop · Start — each with the practice, the line of sight it comes from and its owner

Over four quarters, the pages show whether each line is holding, recovering or drifting, and whether the KISS decisions made at each quarter close moved the next quarter’s results.

Section 5 · Application — In Practice

Our Quarterly Three Lines of Sight

Your group completes the quarterly page for the Key Result of your Capstone organisation and makes its KISS decisions for the next quarter.

Section learning outcomes
  • Exercise forward-looking judgement that holds performance gains across people and organisation.
  • Design a leadership rhythm that sustains the enterprise result quarter after quarter.
5.1 — Application Overview

Your group completes the Quarterly Three Lines of Sight page for the Key Result in your Capstone S2R® Blueprint. Use the worked example in 4.2 as your reference.

Brought into the page from earlier units

  • The Key Result and its WARD — Unit 6, Section 5
  • The ESRG scan — Unit 10 Capstone (10A)
  • The consolidated health position — Unit 11 Capstone (11B)
Capstone work · How Section 5 works
  1. Your group chooses its scribe for this work in the group record bar. The scribe types what your group agrees, and the entries appear on every member’s page.
  2. Part 5.2: the scribe selects “Bring in” to bring your WARD (Unit 6), your ESRG scan (Unit 10) and your consolidated health position (Unit 11) onto the page, then chooses the quarter.
  3. Part 5.3: complete the strategic, operational and behavioural lines, each filtered through WARD.
  4. Part 5.4: record your KISS decisions for the next quarter, with at least one Keep, one Improve, one Stop and one Start.
  5. When your record is complete, each member selects Agree. Your group’s work then goes into your team’s Capstone S2R® Blueprint.
