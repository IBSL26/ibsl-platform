Module 4 · Grounding · Unit 10

ESRG Alignment

Grounding · Pillar 3 of DIG

Grounding establishes the organisational capabilities and healthy operating status required to anchor and sustain strategic performance. It considers how the enterprise is configured to deliver results, the organisational health that sustains performance, and how both are leveraged over time.

The Grounding learning journey progresses from Unit 10 — ESRG Alignment (organisational operating environment), to Unit 11 — Culture Reinforcement & Organisational Health (culture), and then to Unit 12 — Sustaining Performance, which leverages both to sustain high performance.

Module learning outcomes Configure organisational operating conditions to support strategic priorities and enable consistent enterprise performance. Shape organisational culture by establishing and embedding the conditions and practices that strengthen organisational health. Integrate organisational capabilities and cultural practices into a coherent operating environment that supports sustained high performance. Institutionalise organisational performance sustainability through mechanisms that preserve strategic alignment, strengthen adaptability and maintain performance continuity.
Key learning outcomes Design an ESRG-aligned organisational operating environment by establishing the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance requirements necessary to deliver strategic commitments. Develop targeted organisational alignment interventions that address operating constraints and strengthen the organisation’s capacity for consistent strategic delivery.

Section 1 · Awareness — What

What operating conditions must support strategic delivery?

Specifying the organisational capability one Objective requires, domain by domain.

Section learning outcomes
  • Specify the essential organisational operating requirements associated with a selected Objective.
  • Assign each requirement to the appropriate ESRG domain and component, explaining its direct contribution to the intended result.
ESRG Alignment — The Grounding Question

In Unit 6 you met ESRG as the Organisational Operating System: Environment (FIT), Systems (SIP), Resources (AIR) and Governance (PAR), each with three components. There, ESRG answered a performance management question: when results fall short, does the cause lie in people’s performance or in the operating conditions around them?

Unit 10 builds on that foundation. It uses ESRG to ground performance at the level of the Objective: before demand arrives, leaders specify what each component must provide for an Objective to be delivered, and build that capability deliberately. Key Results are reviewed through the year in EPIC (Unit 8); the ESRG review sits one level above them, on the Objective those Key Results serve. Parts 1.1 to 1.4 take each domain in turn and set out, component by component, what must be established for strategic delivery. The executive team tests these requirements once a year, at the annual ESRG review of each Objective.

ESRG Alignment and Strategic Unclogging: When to Use Each

In Unit 9 you used Strategic Unclogging to clear clogs: blockages that slow a Key Result already in execution. You read each clog through its performance outcome, the behaviour around it and the condition behind it, across people performance and organisational performance. You cleared it with a Strategic Unclogging Lever and the Review–Realign–Restore cycle.

ESRG Alignment works on the organisational operating conditions themselves. It starts from an Objective and the demand that Objective places on the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance. Its purpose is to build the organisational capability that carries the strategy. Leaders specify the conditions before demand arrives, score them once a year, at the annual ESRG review, and strengthen them through targeted organisational alignment interventions. Between annual reviews, the clogs that appear in execution are cleared through Strategic Unclogging, so the operating conditions are not rescored every quarter.

The two disciplines feed each other in three ways:

  • When EPIC or Strategic Unclogging traces a Key Result’s shortfall to an organisational condition, that condition becomes an ESRG component to score and strengthen at the next annual review of its Objective.
  • Recurring clogs in the same component show a capability gap the next annual ESRG review must address.
  • A component that scores low at the annual ESRG review shows leaders where clogs are likely to form during the year, so they can watch for them in EPIC and act before Key Results slow.

Illustration: During EPIC, a service team’s response-time Key Result shows it has slipped for three weeks because cases wait between the sales and operations platforms. Strategic Unclogging clears the backlog: leaders realign the handover and restore the response time. At the annual ESRG review of the Objective that Key Result serves, ESRG Alignment shows the same low Integration score in three functions. The executive team then agrees a shared integration requirement so the clog does not return.

1.1 — Environment | FIT

Environment concerns the suitability and reliability of the physical and technological setting in which organisational operations take place.

FITComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
FFacilitiesWhether operating locations and physical facilities are functional and suited to the planned activity.
IInfrastructureWhether essential utilities, connectivity and supporting infrastructure can reliably carry operational demand.
TTechnologyWhether the technological operating environment is available, reliable and fit for the required activity.

Diagnostic question: Are our facilities, infrastructure and technology fit for the strategic requirement?

Illustration: A production expansion depends on stable electrical supply. Repeated outages prevent the additional production capacity from being used consistently.

1.2 — Systems | SIP

Systems concerns how operational work is defined, connected and performed efficiently across the enterprise.

SIPComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
SStandardisationWhether recurring operational processes are clearly defined and consistently applied.
IIntegrationWhether systems and process handoffs connect across functions without avoidable breaks.
PProcess EfficiencyWhether the required output can be delivered without unnecessary steps, delay, duplication or waste.

Diagnostic question: Are our operational processes standardised, integrated and efficient?

Illustration: Customer onboarding follows a documented process, but disconnected sales and finance platforms require repeated manual entry, slowing completion.

1.3 — Resources | AIR

Resources concerns the availability, funding and productive deployment of the organisation’s operational assets.

AIRComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
AAsset AvailabilityWhether sufficient productive equipment and other operating assets are available for the required output.
IInvestmentWhether financial provision is adequate for the strategic and operational commitment.
RResource UtilisationWhether existing assets and funding are allocated and used productively against priorities.

Diagnostic question: Are our assets and financial resources sufficient and effectively utilised?

Illustration: A logistics operation has enough vehicles, but uneven deployment leaves some idle while others are overused, reducing delivery reliability.

1.4 — Governance | PAR

Governance concerns the institutional arrangements through which operational decisions are guided, authorised and exposed risks managed.

PARComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
PPolicies & ProceduresWhether the necessary institutional policies and procedures are established and suitable for the decisions and operations required.
AAuthorityWhether decision rights, delegated authority and approval pathways support timely decisions.
RRisk ManagementWhether material risks to the strategic commitment are identified, assessed, mitigated and monitored.

Diagnostic question: Is our organisational governance up to PAR?

Illustration: Funding for expansion has been approved, yet avoidable approval levels delay the release of expenditure and postpone implementation.

Section 2 · Intelligence — Why

The ESRG Ignition Point: How Each Domain Enables Execution

The four execution-enabling capabilities, and the three dimensions through which an executive team reads its operating conditions together.

Section learning outcomes
  • Translate strategic performance requirements into functional operating capabilities through the four ESRG execution enablers.
  • Distinguish convergence, divergence and dependency in organisational operating conditions to determine the collective response each requires.
2.1 — The ESRG Ignition Point

The ESRG Ignition Point is reached when organisational operating conditions are sufficiently aligned to support the performance demands of the strategy. The four domains make distinct, interdependent contributions to execution:

ESRG domainExecution-enabling capabilityWhat it makes possible
Environment · FITOperational ReadinessProvides a dependable operating setting: facilities accommodate planned activity; essential infrastructure sustains continuity; and technology has the functionality, reliability and capacity to handle strategic demand. This reduces interruptions and protects service or production availability.
Systems · SIPOperational FlowEnables work to progress predictably from initiation to completion: standardised processes create consistency, integrated workflows support handovers across functions, and efficient process design removes avoidable steps, duplication and waiting time. This improves throughput and turnaround time.
Resources · AIRDelivery CapacityMakes the required scale of delivery achievable: productive assets are available when needed, financial investment supports strategic priorities, and existing resources are deployed where demand is greatest. This prevents capacity shortages and avoidable underutilisation.
Governance · PARDecision ResponsivenessAllows organisational action to proceed with clarity and control: policies and procedures guide consistent operations, authority arrangements enable decisions at the right level and time, and risks are identified and managed. This reduces approval bottlenecks while protecting sound decisions.

Ignition principle: A strategic commitment can be delivered consistently only when the operating conditions it depends upon can meet its performance demands.

Illustration: A customer service function has reliable technology and adequate assets, but disconnected handovers delay case resolution. Its constraint is Operational Flow (SIP). Additional equipment would not directly resolve the delay.

Reflect: Which of the four capabilities is most critical to the Objective your function serves, and what would happen if it were unable to meet the required demand?
2.2 — Three Dimensions of Collective ESRG Intelligence

The ESRG Ignition Point in 2.1 is reached for the enterprise only when each execution-enabling capability holds across every function an Objective depends on. Each function experiences Operational Readiness, Operational Flow, Delivery Capacity and Decision Responsiveness from its own position, so one function’s reading of a capability is incomplete. The executive team builds a collective reading by placing the functions’ readings of each capability side by side. Three dimensions of collective intelligence structure that reading. Each dimension looks for one score pattern and calls for one response discipline.

The three dimensions widen the reading in steps, from each function’s own scores to the enterprise as a whole:

Functional Intelligence

Reads each function’s scores for a capability and places them together, so the executive team sees which operating conditions the functions report in common.

Cross-Functional Intelligence

Compares how different functions score the same condition, so the executive team sees where the readings differ and what lies behind the difference.

Enterprise Intelligence

Traces how one function’s operating conditions enable or constrain another function’s delivery and the enterprise’s strategic commitments, so the executive team sees the chain of delivery.

The table links each dimension to what leaders look for in the scanner and what they do about it. The score pattern is the sign in the scores that the dimension is at work. The third column states what the executive team establishes from that pattern, and the response discipline is the kind of response the pattern calls for. Leaders use the table to move from scores to action: once they recognise the pattern a capability shows, they know which response to agree and which functions to bring in.

Intelligence dimensionScore patternWhat the team establishesResponse discipline
Functional IntelligenceConvergence: several functions score the same component or capability alike, high or lowCommon operating strengths or constraints that emerge when the functional assessments are brought togetherCoordinate: one shared response, led by one accountable executive, in place of a separate fix in each function
Cross-Functional IntelligenceDivergence: functions score the same component or capability differentlyDifferences in functional scores or experiences that need explanation and reconciliationReconcile: test the evidence behind each score, establish whether the gap lies in the condition or in how it was scored, and agree the actual requirement
Enterprise IntelligenceDependency: one function’s delivery relies on a capability that another function supplies or controlsHow capabilities in one function enable or constrain other functions and enterprise strategic commitmentsAlign: agree the shared operating requirement, the order of action and what each function commits to the other

Section 3 · Extrapolating — Where

Where Do Executive Biases Distort ESRG Operating Capabilities?

Examining how executive assumptions, functional priorities and decision-making practices influence organisational operating conditions, create execution constraints and affect strategic performance.

Section learning outcomes
  • Diagnose executive decision biases that distort specific ESRG components and weaken the organisational capabilities required for strategic execution.
  • Develop evidence-based debiasing techniques that strengthen executive decision-making and prevent cross-functional operating constraints.
3.1 — The CXO ESRG Bias Contributors

Executive leaders make decisions through the responsibilities, priorities and performance expectations of their portfolios. These perspectives are necessary, but may also determine which operating conditions receive attention, which risks are underestimated and which consequences remain invisible. Each card examines a decision at the point it is made: the approval, investment, sourcing or design choice, the assumption behind it and the ESRG component it weakens.

Section 4 · Integration — Collective

How Does the Executive Team Create Collective Intelligence for ESRG Alignment?

A worked example of the annual ESRG review in three steps: each function scans its operating conditions, the executive team consolidates the scans, then builds its ESRG Alignment Key.

Section learning outcomes
  • Integrate functional ESRG assessments into a collective intelligence view by identifying convergence, divergence and dependency across organisational operating capabilities.
  • Formulate collective intelligence responses that coordinate shared conditions, reconcile functional differences and align operating dependencies affecting strategic commitments.

The worked example: One illustrative company carries out its annual ESRG review; the company and its figures come from no real organisation. Its Objective is to be the most reliable supplier in its market. One of its Key Results, fulfilling customer orders within five working days, is reviewed through the year in EPIC; once a year, the executive team scans ESRG for the Objective. Five functions carry the Objective: Finance, Operations, Commercial, HR and Technology.

The review runs in three steps: Step 1, each function scans its operating conditions (4.1); Step 2, the executive team consolidates the five scans (4.2); Step 3, the executive team builds its ESRG Alignment Key (4.3).

Our company. Your group does this work for the company in your Problem Charter: you set the organisational conditions (environment, systems, resources and governance) your company needs to deliver its Objectives and resolve its central problem. Your company overview, just above the group record bar, shows your company, your trigger, your three problems and your central problem statement.

Organisational Capability Development

The competent leader configures the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance that strategy needs. The organisation gains the capacity to deliver its commitments consistently.

Demonstrable skills

  1. Assess ESRG conditions and correct for executive bias
  2. Interpret ESRG results by execution capability

The unit concept

A strategic commitment can be delivered consistently only when the operating conditions it depends on can meet its performance demands. ESRG Alignment grounds organisational performance at the level of the Objective: before demand arrives, leaders specify what each component of the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance must provide, test it once a year at the annual ESRG review, and build the capability deliberately. Each domain enables execution through one capability: Operational Readiness, Operational Flow, Delivery Capacity and Decision Responsiveness. Because each function reads these capabilities from its own position, the executive team builds a collective reading through convergence, divergence and dependency, and responds by coordinating, reconciling or aligning.

3. The ESRG Ignition Point

The Ignition Point is reached when operating conditions are aligned enough to support the performance demands of the strategy. Each domain makes a distinct, interdependent contribution:

DomainExecution-enabling capabilityWhat it makes possible
Environment · FITOperational ReadinessA dependable operating setting that reduces interruptions and protects service or production availability
Systems · SIPOperational FlowWork that progresses predictably from initiation to completion, improving throughput and turnaround
Resources · AIRDelivery CapacityThe required scale of delivery, preventing capacity shortages and avoidable underutilisation
Governance · PARDecision ResponsivenessAction that proceeds with clarity and control, reducing approval bottlenecks while protecting sound decisions

Ignition principle: a strategic commitment can be delivered consistently only when the operating conditions it depends on can meet its performance demands. An intervention must address the capability that is actually constraining delivery.

4. Three dimensions of collective ESRG intelligence

The Ignition Point holds for the enterprise only when each capability holds across every function an Objective depends on. The executive team places the functions' readings side by side:

DimensionWhat it readsScore patternWhat the team establishesResponse discipline
Functional IntelligenceEach function's scores placed togetherConvergence: several functions score the same component alike, high or lowCommon operating strengths or constraintsCoordinate: one shared response led by one accountable executive, in place of separate fixes
Cross-Functional IntelligenceHow different functions score the same conditionDivergence: functions score the same component differentlyDifferences that need explanation and reconciliationReconcile: test the evidence behind each score, establish whether the gap lies in the condition or in the scoring, and agree the actual requirement
Enterprise IntelligenceHow one function's conditions enable or constrain another's deliveryDependency: one function relies on a capability another supplies or controlsThe chain of delivery across functions and enterprise commitmentsAlign: agree the shared operating requirement, the order of action and what each function commits to the other

A difference in ratings does not by itself prove misalignment, and a shared low rating does not by itself prove a shared root cause: executive dialogue validates both before the response is agreed.

5. The annual ESRG review

  1. Each function scans its operating conditions. Each functional leader scores the 12 components for the Objective from evidence of how policies, systems and plans perform in practice, on a 1–5 scale: 1 Critical constraint (materially prevents required performance); 2 Significant gap (regularly disrupts or limits performance); 3 Partially effective (supports basic operations, not strategic demand); 4 Effective (reliably supports current requirements); 5 Fully aligned (supports current and anticipated demand).
  2. The executive team consolidates the scans. Each function's scores become one column; each capability score is the average of its three components, and the enterprise average is the average across functions. Components that do not apply are recorded as N/A. A reasonable enterprise average can conceal a critical low score in a function the Objective depends on.
  3. The executive team builds its ESRG Alignment Key. For each execution-enabling capability, it reads convergence, divergence and dependency, agrees its Collective Intelligence Response (Coordinate, Reconcile or Align) and names the executive functions involved. The Key is the commitment for the year.
