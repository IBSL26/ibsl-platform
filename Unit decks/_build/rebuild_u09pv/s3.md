# Where Do Executive Decisions and Practices Contribute to Performance Clogs

Examining how decisions and management practices within executive functions influence people performance, organisational conditions and strategic results.

## Section learning outcomes

-   Reveal how executive decisions and practices can create or reinforce performance clogs.

-   Examine how decisions within one executive function affect contributions and performance across the organisation.

## Facilitator Guidance Extrapolating

Connect this section to the seven clogs and Three Performance Lines of Sight introduced in Section 2. Explain that every executive function shapes the conditions in which other teams work. Decisions in finance, operations, people management, technology and commercial functions can affect performance beyond their own portfolios.

Use the CXO cards to illustrate a decision or practice, the organisational result affected, how people respond and the clog that can follow. Begin with participants’ own roles, then invite them to explore two closely connected functions. Participants use the final reflection to examine a decision or practice from their own organisation.

## 3.1 The CXO Performance Clog Contributors

Executive roles carry responsibility for different parts of the organisation’s operating system. Through their decisions, executives determine how resources are allocated, work is organised, people are managed, performance is measured and risks are controlled. These decisions shape the conditions in which individuals and teams contribute to strategic results.

A performance clog can form when a decision or management practice in one executive function creates difficulties for people working elsewhere. Select an executive role to examine a realistic decision and its consequences through the Three Performance Lines of Sight.

### CEO Enterprise Direction

**Executive decision or practice.** The CEO repeatedly postpones a decision about which of two competing strategic initiatives should receive priority.

**Line of Sight 1 — Performance Outcome.** Both initiatives fall behind their planned delivery milestones.

**Line of Sight 2 — Performance Behaviour.** Executive teams continue pursuing competing priorities. Managers divide resources between both initiatives and delay commitments while awaiting direction.

**Line of Sight 3 — Performance Condition.** Leadership Avoidance. The unresolved trade-off sustains conflicting priorities and prevents resources and attention from being concentrated on an agreed direction.

**Question for participants.** Which unresolved strategic trade-off may be causing competing priorities across the organisation?

### CFO Financial Stewardship

**Executive decision or practice.** The CFO introduces additional senior-level approvals for routine operational expenditure.

**Line of Sight 1 — Performance Outcome.** Operational delivery milestones are repeatedly missed despite adequate budget provision.

**Line of Sight 2 — Performance Behaviour.** Managers wait for purchasing approval, employees delay scheduled work, and teams frequently escalate routine expenditure requests.

**Line of Sight 3 — Performance Condition.** Decision Bottlenecks. Financial authority is positioned too far from routine operational decisions, slowing the flow of work.

**Question for participants.** Where might our financial controls be delaying decisions needed for operational delivery?

### COO Operational Discipline

**Executive decision or practice.** The COO maintains separate departmental responsibilities for activities that require coordinated end-to-end delivery.

**Line of Sight 1 — Performance Outcome.** Customer service resolution times remain below the required standard.

**Line of Sight 2 — Performance Behaviour.** Teams complete their assigned tasks but transfer unresolved cases between departments. Employees spend time following up on work controlled by other teams.

**Line of Sight 3 — Performance Condition.** Structural Misfit. The operating arrangement fragments ownership of the customer outcome and creates delays at functional boundaries.

**Question for participants.** Which operating arrangements make it difficult for teams to deliver shared outcomes?

### CHRO People and Culture

**Executive decision or practice.** The CHRO maintains individual performance measures focused on task completion when the strategy requires greater cross-functional collaboration.

**Line of Sight 1 — Performance Outcome.** Functional targets are achieved while shared enterprise outcomes remain weak.

**Line of Sight 2 — Performance Behaviour.** Employees prioritise their own scorecards and give less attention to activities that support other teams or shared results.

**Line of Sight 3 — Performance Condition.** Misaligned Metrics & Incentives. The performance management system reinforces individual outputs without sufficient recognition of contributions to enterprise outcomes.

**Question for participants.** Which performance measures may be reinforcing behaviours that undermine collaboration?

### CTO/CIO Technology and Information

**Executive decision or practice.** The CTO/CIO approves separate technology platforms without sufficiently addressing the integration required between functions.

**Line of Sight 1 — Performance Outcome.** Service turnaround times and management information reliability deteriorate.

**Line of Sight 2 — Performance Behaviour.** Employees duplicate information across systems, reconcile conflicting records and rely on manual updates.

**Line of Sight 3 — Performance Condition.** Technology / Infrastructure Lag. Fragmented information systems make routine work slower and reduce the visibility needed for coordinated delivery.

**Question for participants.** Where are technology limitations forcing employees to rely on workarounds?

### CMO Market and Customer Insight

**Executive decision or practice.** The CMO increases promotional activity without confirming whether the organisation can fulfil the resulting demand.

**Line of Sight 1 — Performance Outcome.** Customer complaints increase despite improved campaign performance.

**Line of Sight 2 — Performance Behaviour.** Customer-facing employees make commitments that fulfilment teams struggle to meet. Teams spend more time managing complaints and expectations.

**Line of Sight 3 — Performance Condition.** Structural Misfit. Market-facing activity and delivery capacity are insufficiently coordinated, creating strain across the customer value chain.

**Question for participants.** Where might our market-facing decisions be creating delivery expectations that other functions cannot meet?

### CCO Commercial Performance

**Executive decision or practice.** The CCO introduces aggressive sales-volume incentives without adequately incorporating delivery quality and customer retention.

**Line of Sight 1 — Performance Outcome.** Sales volumes increase while margins and customer retention deteriorate.

**Line of Sight 2 — Performance Behaviour.** Commercial teams prioritise closing transactions and offer commitments that place pressure on fulfilment teams.

**Line of Sight 3 — Performance Condition.** Misaligned Metrics & Incentives. The incentive system rewards immediate commercial output without sufficient attention to sustainable customer and enterprise value.

**Question for participants.** Which commercial targets might encourage short-term gains that weaken overall results?

### CPO Procurement and Supply

**Executive decision or practice.** The CPO prioritises lowest purchase price without sufficiently considering reliability and operational quality requirements.

**Line of Sight 1 — Performance Outcome.** Production interruptions and quality failures increase despite reported procurement savings.

**Line of Sight 2 — Performance Behaviour.** Operational teams spend time resolving supply problems, replacing unsuitable inputs and adjusting production schedules.

**Line of Sight 3 — Performance Condition.** Misaligned Metrics & Incentives. Procurement performance is evaluated through savings while the cost of unreliable supply is experienced elsewhere.

**Question for participants.** Where might procurement measures be encouraging savings that increase costs or delays in other functions?

### CRO Risk and Governance

**Executive decision or practice.** The CRO requires the same extensive approval process for routine low-risk changes as for significant strategic decisions.

**Line of Sight 1 — Performance Outcome.** Operational improvements and responses to emerging issues are repeatedly delayed.

**Line of Sight 2 — Performance Behaviour.** Managers defer small improvements, submit repeated approval requests and avoid making decisions within their operational responsibilities.

**Line of Sight 3 — Performance Condition.** Decision Bottlenecks. Governance requirements are disproportionate to the decisions involved, slowing action and weakening ownership.

**Question for participants.** Which approval requirements may be slowing routine decisions without materially improving risk control?

### CSO Strategic Integration

**Executive decision or practice.** The CSO continues reporting strategic initiative progress without adequately examining unresolved dependencies between functions.

**Line of Sight 1 — Performance Outcome.** Strategic milestones repeatedly slip despite satisfactory functional progress reports.

**Line of Sight 2 — Performance Behaviour.** Functional leaders report their own activities while cross-functional obstacles remain unresolved.

**Line of Sight 3 — Performance Condition.** Structural Misfit. The strategy review process does not sufficiently surface fragmented contribution ownership and delivery dependencies.

**Question for participants.** Which interdependencies remain unresolved despite regular strategic progress reporting?

## Closing Insight

Executive decisions shape the conditions in which people and teams perform. The Three Performance Lines of Sight help leaders recognise how decisions within their own functions may influence results elsewhere in the organisation.

Section 4 — Integration will focus on how executive leaders work collectively to remove performance clogs and restore strategic coherence and performance momentum.
