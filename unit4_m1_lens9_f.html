Module 4 · Grounding · Unit 10

ESRG Alignment

Grounding · Pillar 3 of DIG

Grounding establishes the organisational capabilities and healthy operating status required to anchor and sustain strategic performance. It considers how the enterprise is configured to deliver results, the organisational health that sustains performance, and how both are leveraged over time.

The Grounding learning journey progresses from Unit 10 — ESRG Alignment (organisational operating environment), to Unit 11 — Culture Reinforcement & Organisational Health (culture), and then to Unit 12 — Sustaining Performance, which leverages both to sustain high performance.

Module learning outcomes Configure organisational operating conditions to support strategic priorities and enable consistent enterprise performance. Shape organisational culture by establishing and embedding the conditions and practices that strengthen organisational health. Integrate organisational capabilities and cultural practices into a coherent operating environment that supports sustained high performance. Institutionalise organisational performance sustainability through mechanisms that preserve strategic alignment, strengthen adaptability and maintain performance continuity.
Key learning outcomes Design an ESRG-aligned organisational operating environment by establishing the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance requirements necessary to deliver strategic commitments. Develop targeted organisational alignment interventions that address operating constraints and strengthen the organisation’s capacity for consistent strategic delivery.

Section 1 · Awareness — What

What operating conditions must support strategic delivery?

Specifying the organisational capability one strategic commitment requires, domain by domain.

Section learning outcomes
  • Specify the essential organisational operating requirements associated with a selected strategic commitment.
  • Assign each requirement to the appropriate ESRG domain and component, explaining its direct contribution to the intended result.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Awareness

Begin with one strategic commitment from the capstone. Ask: “What must be in place within the organisation for this result to be delivered?” Recall the four ESRG domains and their three components each from Unit 6. Ask participants to identify only the operating requirements that materially affect their chosen commitment. At this stage, the task is to define and locate requirements; detailed evidence testing follows in Intelligence.

ESRG Alignment — The Grounding Question

A strategy places demands on the organisation’s operating environment. Participants met ESRG in Unit 6 as the organisational operating system: Environment (FIT), Systems (SIP), Resources (AIR) and Governance (PAR), each with three components. Unit 10 uses ESRG to develop the organisational capability that grounds performance. Section 1 starts with one strategic commitment from the group’s capstone work: which components its delivery depends on, and what each of those components must provide for the result to be delivered.

ESRG Alignment and Strategic Unclogging: When to Use Each

In Unit 9 participants used Strategic Unclogging to clear clogs: blockages that slow a Key Result already in execution. They read each clog through its performance outcome, the behaviour around it and the condition behind it, across people performance and organisational performance. They cleared it with a Strategic Unclogging Lever and the Review–Realign–Restore cycle.

ESRG Alignment works on the organisational operating conditions themselves. It starts from a strategic commitment and the demand that commitment places on the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance. Its purpose is to build the organisational capability that carries the strategy. Leaders specify the conditions before demand arrives, score them on a fixed schedule and strengthen them through targeted organisational alignment interventions.

Strategic UncloggingESRG Alignment
Use it whenA Key Result is slowing and friction signals show in results or behaviourA strategic commitment is approved, launched or scaled, and at each annual baseline and quarterly recalibration
Question it answersWhat is blocking this result, and how do we clear it?Can our operating conditions carry this commitment, and where must we build capability?
What it coversPeople performance and organisational performanceOrganisational operating conditions (ESRG)
Leaders’ responseSelect an Unclogging Lever and run Review–Realign–RestoreCoordinate, reconcile or align, and design targeted organisational alignment interventions

The two disciplines feed each other in three ways:

  • A clog traced to an organisational condition becomes an ESRG component to score and strengthen.
  • Recurring clogs in the same component show a capability gap the next annual baseline must address.
  • A component that scores low at a quarterly recalibration shows leaders where clogs are likely to form, so they can act before Key Results slow.

Illustration: A service team’s response-time Key Result slips for three weeks because cases wait between the sales and operations platforms. Strategic Unclogging clears the backlog: leaders realign the handover and restore the response time. At the next recalibration, ESRG Alignment shows the same low Integration score in three functions. The executive team then agrees a shared integration requirement so the clog does not return.

1.1 — Environment | FIT

Environment concerns the suitability and reliability of the physical and technological setting in which organisational operations take place.

FITComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
FFacilitiesWhether operating locations and physical facilities are functional and suited to the planned activity.
IInfrastructureWhether essential utilities, connectivity and supporting infrastructure can reliably carry operational demand.
TTechnologyWhether the technological operating environment is available, reliable and fit for the required activity.

Diagnostic question: Are our facilities, infrastructure and technology fit for the strategic requirement?

Illustration: A production expansion depends on stable electrical supply. Repeated outages prevent the additional production capacity from being used consistently.

1.2 — Systems | SIP

Systems concerns how operational work is defined, connected and performed efficiently across the enterprise.

SIPComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
SStandardisationWhether recurring operational processes are clearly defined and consistently applied.
IIntegrationWhether systems and process handoffs connect across functions without avoidable breaks.
PProcess EfficiencyWhether the required output can be delivered without unnecessary steps, delay, duplication or waste.

Diagnostic question: Are our operational processes standardised, integrated and efficient?

Illustration: Customer onboarding follows a documented process, but disconnected sales and finance platforms require repeated manual entry, slowing completion.

1.3 — Resources | AIR

Resources concerns the availability, funding and productive deployment of the organisation’s operational assets.

AIRComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
AAsset AvailabilityWhether sufficient productive equipment and other operating assets are available for the required output.
IInvestmentWhether financial provision is adequate for the strategic and operational commitment.
RResource UtilisationWhether existing assets and funding are allocated and used productively against priorities.

Diagnostic question: Are our assets and financial resources sufficient and effectively utilised?

Illustration: A logistics operation has enough vehicles, but uneven deployment leaves some idle while others are overused, reducing delivery reliability.

1.4 — Governance | PAR

Governance concerns the institutional arrangements through which operational decisions are guided, authorised and exposed risks managed.

PARComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
PPolicies & ProceduresWhether the necessary institutional policies and procedures are established and suitable for the decisions and operations required.
AAuthorityWhether decision rights, delegated authority and approval pathways support timely decisions.
RRisk ManagementWhether material risks to the strategic commitment are identified, assessed, mitigated and monitored.

Diagnostic question: Is our organisational governance up to PAR?

Illustration: Funding for expansion has been approved, yet avoidable approval levels delay the release of expenditure and postpone implementation.

Section 2 · Intelligence — Why

The ESRG Ignition Point: How Each Domain Enables Execution

The four execution-enabling capabilities, and a scanner that profiles how well a function’s operating conditions support its strategic commitments.

Section learning outcomes
  • Translate strategic performance requirements into functional operating capabilities through the four ESRG execution enablers.
  • Build and maintain an evidence-based functional ESRG performance profile to identify operating strengths, constraints and changes in alignment over time.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Intelligence

In Awareness, participants specified the operating conditions their strategic commitments require and located them within FIT, SIP, AIR and PAR. Intelligence now asks whether those conditions enable the level of performance demanded. Introduce the four execution-enabling capabilities, then have participants score their own function. Use the same scanner for the annual baseline and quarterly recalibration; detailed root-cause diagnosis belongs in Extrapolating.

2.1 — The ESRG Ignition Point

The ESRG Ignition Point is reached when organisational operating conditions are sufficiently aligned to support the performance demands of the strategy. The four domains make distinct, interdependent contributions to execution:

ESRG domainExecution-enabling capabilityWhat it makes possible
Environment · FITOperational ReadinessProvides a dependable operating setting: facilities accommodate planned activity; essential infrastructure sustains continuity; and technology has the functionality, reliability and capacity to handle strategic demand. This reduces interruptions and protects service or production availability.
Systems · SIPOperational FlowEnables work to progress predictably from initiation to completion: standardised processes create consistency, integrated workflows support handovers across functions, and efficient process design removes avoidable steps, duplication and waiting time. This improves throughput and turnaround time.
Resources · AIRDelivery CapacityMakes the required scale of delivery achievable: productive assets are available when needed, financial investment supports strategic priorities, and existing resources are deployed where demand is greatest. This prevents capacity shortages and avoidable underutilisation.
Governance · PARDecision ResponsivenessAllows organisational action to proceed with clarity and control: policies and procedures guide consistent operations, authority arrangements enable decisions at the right level and time, and risks are identified and managed. This reduces approval bottlenecks while protecting sound decisions.

Ignition principle: A strategic commitment can be delivered consistently only when the operating conditions it depends upon can meet its performance demands.

Illustration: A customer service function has reliable technology and adequate assets, but disconnected handovers delay case resolution. Its constraint is Operational Flow (SIP). Additional equipment would not directly resolve the delay.

Which of the four capabilities is most critical to your function’s current strategic commitment, and what would happen if it were unable to meet the required demand?

Section 3 · Extrapolating — Where

Where Do Executive Biases Distort ESRG Operating Capabilities?

Examining how executive assumptions, functional priorities and decision-making practices influence organisational operating conditions, create execution constraints and affect strategic performance.

Section learning outcomes
  • Diagnose executive decision biases that distort specific ESRG components and weaken the organisational capabilities required for strategic execution.
  • Develop evidence-based debiasing techniques that strengthen executive decision-making and prevent cross-functional operating constraints.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Extrapolating

Connect this section to the four execution-enabling capabilities introduced in Intelligence: Environment — Operational Readiness; Systems — Operational Flow; Resources — Delivery Capacity; Governance — Decision Responsiveness.

Executives influence these capabilities through decisions within their functional portfolios. Legitimate functional priorities can introduce assumptions that affect operating conditions beyond the executive’s immediate portfolio. A decision intended to improve one function may inadvertently constrain another.

For example, a CFO may reduce expenditure to protect financial performance while weakening investment required for operational continuity. A COO may standardise procedures to improve consistency while introducing unnecessary delays.

Central question: Where might my executive decisions and assumptions be weakening the organisational operating capabilities required to deliver our strategy?

◆ Facilitator Approach

For each CXO card: introduce the bias and decision; examine the two ESRG components most directly affected; trace the execution consequences; and discuss the evidence or decision adjustment required to counter the bias. Invite participants to challenge the examples and identify alternative decisions that preserve functional objectives while strengthening enterprise performance. Examples are illustrative.

3.1 — The CXO ESRG Bias Contributors

Executive leaders make decisions through the responsibilities, priorities and performance expectations of their portfolios. These perspectives are necessary, but may also determine which operating conditions receive attention, which risks are underestimated and which consequences remain invisible. Each card examines a decision at the point it is made: the approval, investment, sourcing or design choice, the assumption behind it and the ESRG component it weakens.

Section 4 · Integration — Collective

How Does the Executive Team Create Collective Intelligence for ESRG Alignment?

Combining functional operating assessments into a shared executive understanding of common conditions, differences between functions and the dependencies that determine strategic delivery.

Section learning outcomes
  • Integrate functional ESRG assessments into a collective intelligence view by identifying convergence, divergence and dependency across organisational operating capabilities.
  • Formulate collective intelligence responses that coordinate shared conditions, reconcile functional differences and align operating dependencies affecting strategic commitments.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Integration

In Intelligence, participants learned the four execution-enabling capabilities and the Functional ESRG Scanner. In Extrapolating, they examined executive biases that can distort operating decisions. Integration brings functional perspectives together to establish what the executive team can see, understand and address collectively.

Use the illustrative, prepopulated Enterprise ESRG Scanner in this section to teach interpretation and response. Participants do not rescore the illustrative data. In 4.5, each participant completes the Functional ESRG Scanner for their assigned capstone executive function. Those actual scores feed the Enterprise Scanner for the capstone work in Section 5, where the team validates findings and determines its organisational interventions.

Central question: What can the executive team discover about the operating environment collectively that no function can establish from its own assessment alone?

4.1 — Three Dimensions of Collective ESRG Intelligence

Collective intelligence progresses through three connected dimensions. Each is associated with one primary interpretive perspective and a corresponding response discipline. The pairing is a practical learning sequence; each perspective can also be applied at the other levels.

Intelligence dimensionPrimary perspectiveWhat the team establishesGuiding questionResponse discipline
Functional IntelligenceConvergenceCommon operating strengths or constraints emerging when individual functional assessments are brought together.Where are our functions pointing to the same operating conditions?Coordinate
Cross-Functional IntelligenceDivergenceDifferences in functional scores or experiences that need explanation and reconciliation.Where do our functional assessments differ, and why?Reconcile
Enterprise IntelligenceDependencyHow capabilities in one function enable or constrain other functions and enterprise strategic commitments.Where do we depend on one another to deliver enterprise results?Align

The executive conversation progresses: What do we have in common? → Where do we differ? → How do we depend on one another?

Convergence — Functional Intelligence

When multiple functions independently report a similar condition, the executive team gains evidence of a potentially shared operating pattern. For example, low Integration scores in Commercial, Operations and Technology may indicate a recurring difficulty connecting workflows. Convergence invites the team to test whether the weakness has a common cause and whether a coordinated response is warranted.

Divergence — Cross-Functional Intelligence

Differences in scores invite investigation before any judgement is made. Technology may report that its systems are reliable while Operations reports that the same technological environment does not adequately support required activities. The executive team examines the basis of each assessment, distinguishes differing operating experiences from inconsistent criteria and reconciles the actual requirement.

Dependency — Enterprise Intelligence

A function may score strongly while its strategic contribution remains exposed to another function’s operating weakness. Commercial growth, for example, may depend on Operations infrastructure and Finance investment. Dependency makes the chain of delivery visible and enables executives to agree shared operating requirements and commitments.

◆ Facilitator prompts — for each execution-enabling capability
  • Convergence: Which functions report similar conditions, and do those ratings reflect a shared cause?
  • Divergence: What explains the differences in scores, requirements or operating experience?
  • Dependency: Which strategic commitment depends on these conditions, and which functions must act together?
◆ Facilitator caution

A difference in ratings does not by itself prove misalignment; a shared low rating does not by itself prove a shared root cause. Executive dialogue must validate both.

Section 5 · Application — In Practice

The Enterprise ESRG Alignment Clinic

From individual functional assessments to enterprise intelligence, collective responses and implementable operating interventions.

Section learning outcomes
  • Consolidate and interpret functional ESRG assessments to establish an evidence-based enterprise view of the operating capabilities required for strategic delivery.
  • Develop and operationalise collective ESRG alignment interventions that address operating constraints and strengthen cross-functional delivery of strategic commitments.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDE — Overview

Participants work as the executive team of their assigned capstone organisation. Each member brings the Functional ESRG Performance Scanner completed for their assigned function in Section 4.5. The team consolidates those existing scores into the Enterprise ESRG Scanner, interprets them by execution-enabling capability, agrees a Collective Intelligence Response and then transfers each agreed response into an ESRG Alignment Intervention Plan.

It is now Q1. The executive team meets for its first quarterly ESRG review, working from the annual baseline each member completed in Section 4.5.

Core application question: What must this executive team collectively strengthen in the organisation’s operating conditions to deliver its strategic commitments consistently?

Inputs: Capstone strategy and commitments; individual functional scanners; linked Enterprise ESRG Scanner; Collective Intelligence Response Table; ESRG Alignment Intervention Plan.

Non-negotiable: No rescoring at enterprise level. Component scores are pulled directly from the functional scanners. The executive team may query the evidence behind a score, but any correction is made in the originating functional assessment and flows through to the enterprise view.
