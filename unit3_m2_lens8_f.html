Unit 9 — Session Overview

Orientation, facilitation questions, tone guidance, and watch points for the full unit.

◆ Unit Intent

Unit 9 sits at a pivotal moment in the S2R® climb. By this point, the strategy is clear, performance expectations are set, and commitments have been secured. Now the gears begin grinding. This unit names that friction precisely — and gives the leadership team the engineering vocabulary and diagnostic discipline to address it.

A clog is a signal. It indicates where the organisation has not yet adapted to the new strategic direction, in the performance of individuals and teams and in the organisational conditions that support the execution of their tasks. Leaders who understand this stop asking “Why are we failing?” and start asking “Where are our people and our organisational conditions not yet ready to carry the strategy?”

A clog shows in people performance and in organisational performance. In people performance, individuals and teams stall on their KPIs: decisions wait, effort pulls in different directions, and tasks are completed while the Key Result does not move. In organisational performance, the organisational conditions do not support the execution of the individuals’ tasks. The diagnosis of every clog examines both sides before a lever is selected.

The Strategic Unclogging Clinic in Application is the centrepiece: each group returns to an original work-based challenge, identifies the clog, selects the lever and applies Review–Realign–Restore within its Capstone organisation. The quality of the diagnostic conversation is the measure of this unit.

  • “When you last named a performance gap, did you name the symptom or the root cause? What’s the difference in your context?”
  • “Which of the seven clogs is most predictable in your organisation given your current strategic direction? What does that tell you about where to look first?”

◆ Tone & Watch Points

Teach past recognition. When leaders first encounter the clog map, they often feel relieved: they recognise their organisations immediately. Take them from recognition to diagnosis and intervention: “Recognising it is the start. Now name it precisely and select the lever.”

Teach the test for Cultural Resistance. It is the most comfortable diagnosis because it externalises the problem. Teach the question that tests it: “Is this a culture issue, or is it the downstream consequence of Leadership Avoidance?”

Teach what a lever is. “We need to improve communication” is too vague to act on. A lever is a structural adjustment: a redesigned decision right, a reset KPI, a rebuilt process, a named accountability.

Teach both sides of every clog. Leaders tend to read a stalled KPI as a people problem, because individuals’ performance is reviewed every month and the organisational conditions seldom are. For each clog, take them to the second question: do the organisational conditions support the execution of the individuals’ tasks?

1.1 — What is a Strategic Clog?
Core concept

At this point, the strategy is clear. Objectives are defined. Performance expectations are in motion. Each Key Result has been translated into KPIs that individuals and teams deliver. Leaders have agreed the organisational conditions that support the execution of those individuals’ tasks. A rhythm of review tracks the progress of both.

Then the gears begin grinding.

Progress slows on a Key Result that everyone supports. Reviews record effort, explanations and revised dates, and the result does not move. The plan still stands. What holds it back is a Strategic Clog.

At its simplest, a Strategic Clog is an internal blockage that prevents strategic intent from flowing into real-world execution. It is the widening gap between what we said we would do and what is actually happening. These are fundamental misalignments where the organisation’s current state — its habits, its structures, its very DNA — works actively against its future goals.

Strategy sets the destination. Flow determines whether you have the power to arrive.

To navigate this phase of the execution climb, leadership must evolve. Designing the strategy starts the work. The role of the leadership team is to become Engineers of Execution Flow — developing the diagnostic eyes to detect clogs early, before they compound into stalled momentum or declining performance.

Every clog interferes with one or more elements of ABCV Directional Integrity:

  • Arena — clarity of the problem space and customer end-game
  • Boundaries — structural, operational, and regulatory constraints
  • Competition — how the organisation defines alternatives and threats
  • Value Proposition — how the organisation creates distinct value

When execution is clogged, strategy loses directional integrity — gradually and silently.

System Logic: A clog acts like a pressure distortion point in the system. It redirects effort away from intended outcomes, weakens coherence across functions, and creates misinterpretation of strategic intent.

Where clogs form

Strategic intent reaches the Key Result through the tasks that individuals and teams execute. Those tasks depend on the organisational conditions around them. These conditions are the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance (ESRG): the facilities, infrastructure and technology the work depends on, the processes that carry it across functions, the assets and funding that make delivery possible, and the policies, decision authority and risk management that guide it. A clog can form in either, and it usually acts on both.

People performance

A clog shows in the performance of individuals and teams. Decisions wait, effort pulls in different directions, and tasks are completed while the KPI holds still. The review sees this first, because the individuals’ numbers are reviewed.

Organisational performance

A clog shows when the organisational conditions no longer support the execution of the individuals’ tasks, as the strategy places new weight on them. Organisational performance seldom carries a number of its own, so it stays unseen until leaders ask for it.

◆ PARTICIPANT ACTIVITY

Participants write a reflection in their own file: a strategy initiative that stalled in their organisation, the blockage behind it, whether it could have been named earlier, and the organisational condition the individuals’ tasks depended on.

1.2 — Understanding Where Performance Becomes Constrained
Why clogs form · Two dimensions of performance

When leaders define a strategy, they set a new direction for the organisation. The organisation executes that direction through what it has today: its structure, decision rights, culture, measures, capabilities and technology, and the ways of working its people have built within them. Where any of these has not yet adapted to the strategy, friction appears.

Clogs form where the strategy stretches today’s organisation beyond what it was designed to carry. A strategy that requires rapid coordination across functions exposes Structural Misfit. A strategy that depends on data-driven decisions reveals Capability Gaps. A strategy that requires decisions closer to the customer exposes Decision Bottlenecks. Each strategy places its weight on known parts of the organisation, so leaders can anticipate where its clogs will form.

Experienced leadership teams read a clog as a signal of where the organisation must adapt next. Setting the direction is one part of their role; ensuring the organisation can carry it is the other. To read the signal, leaders examine where performance becomes constrained.

Strategy depends on the organisation’s ability to convert its strategic intent into coordinated contributions and measurable results. This requires two connected dimensions of performance:

Organisational performance

The enterprise’s ability to create, deliver and sustain value. It reflects how effectively structures, processes, resources, capabilities, governance and operating systems work together to produce strategic outcomes.

People performance

How effectively individuals and teams understand expectations, make decisions, exercise judgement, apply capabilities, collaborate and deliver their contributions to organisational results.

Both dimensions operate within the same enterprise system. When organisational conditions are weak, even capable and committed people may struggle to perform. They experience delays, conflicting priorities, unclear responsibilities, resource shortages and barriers beyond their authority to resolve.

When people performance is weak, the organisation may have adequate resources and well-designed processes but still experience poor judgement, inconsistent behaviours, weak collaboration, limited ownership and unreliable delivery.

These conditions often reinforce one another. For example, unclear decision rights create hesitation among managers. Repeated hesitation then encourages senior leaders to centralise decisions further, increasing the original bottleneck.

The 7-Clog Diagnostic Map identifies seven recurring conditions that constrain the organisation’s capacity to deliver its strategy. The diagnostic examines both the condition of the organisational system and the performance patterns emerging within it.

Participants write a reflection in their own file: the element of their operating system most likely to create friction under their current strategic direction, and why.

1.3 — The Seven Performance Clogs
ABCV-aligned · Select each clog to explore

Participants select each clog to examine its organisational conditions, effects on people, observable patterns, early warnings and strategic consequences.

Clog 01
Structural Misfit
Boundaries
▼
Organisational performance · System reality

The organisational design no longer supports the work required by the strategy. Roles, reporting relationships, governance arrangements, accountability boundaries and functional interfaces may still reflect earlier business priorities. Work requiring integrated contributions is divided across departments without sufficient coordination or shared ownership.

People performance · Contribution reality

Employees may understand their individual responsibilities but struggle to see how their contributions connect to the enterprise outcome. Managers can become protective of functional territory, while teams experience competing instructions and repeated handoffs. Performance suffers when people are held accountable for outcomes they cannot deliver without decisions or inputs controlled elsewhere.

Observable performance patterns
  • Duplication of work across functions
  • Repeated bottlenecks at functional handoffs
  • Fragmented ownership of strategic outcomes
  • Front-office and back-office coordination failures
  • Conflicting priorities between departments
Early warning signals
  • Increasing time spent coordinating routine work
  • Greater dependence on informal relationships and workarounds
  • Repeated escalation of cross-functional disagreements
  • Performance gaps concentrated at handoff points
Strategic impact: Resources and effort are absorbed by internal friction. Individuals may achieve their functional targets while enterprise outcomes remain weak. The organisation loses the ability to coordinate delivery across its full value chain.
Diagnostic question: Are results being constrained by how responsibilities, authority and work are organised?
Worked example

A customer service team is measured on response time, but it depends on operations and finance to resolve enquiries. No function owns the complete customer-resolution outcome. Staff work hard, yet customer complaints continue.

Clog 02
Capability Gaps
Arena
▼
Organisational performance · System reality

The organisation lacks, or has uneven access to, the capabilities required to succeed in its chosen strategic arena. Gaps may involve technical expertise, management capacity, specialised equipment, operating processes, digital competence, partnerships, funding or the ability to scale. Existing capabilities may support current operations but remain insufficient for the new strategic ambition.

People performance · Contribution reality

Individuals may lack the skills, experience, knowledge, tools or support needed to meet new performance expectations. Others may possess strong capabilities but be deployed in roles that underuse them. Repeated reliance on a few specialists creates overload and makes delivery vulnerable. Without adequate development and support, employees can lose confidence, improvise unsuitable methods or avoid unfamiliar tasks.

Observable performance patterns
  • Strategic initiatives stall despite clear objectives
  • Repeated rework and inconsistent quality
  • Critical work depends on a small number of specialists
  • Similar teams produce widely different performance results
  • Outsourcing becomes necessary for strategically essential work
Early warning signals
  • Training needs persist across several review cycles
  • Key employees carry unsustainable workloads
  • New initiatives repeatedly miss readiness milestones
  • Capability gaps emerge when operations expand
Strategic impact: The enterprise cannot consistently deliver the value promised by its strategic positioning. People face expectations that exceed available capability, and the organisation struggles to compete, scale or sustain improved results.
Diagnostic question: Does the organisation possess and deploy the capabilities necessary to operate successfully in its chosen arena?
Worked example

A healthcare provider introduces a digital patient-care model, but staff lack the necessary digital skills and the organisation has insufficient technical support. Service quality varies, adoption slows and employees become frustrated.

Clog 03
Decision Bottlenecks
Boundaries
▼
Organisational performance · System reality

Decision rights, delegation limits, approval processes or governance layers prevent decisions from being made at the appropriate level and speed. Authority is concentrated away from the people closest to the relevant information or delivery responsibility. Escalation processes do not consistently distinguish strategic decisions from routine operational decisions.

People performance · Contribution reality

Managers become hesitant to exercise judgement because they are uncertain about their authority or fear being blamed for decisions. Teams learn to seek approval for matters they could reasonably resolve. Senior leaders become overloaded, while middle managers lose initiative and confidence. Accountability becomes difficult to enforce where responsibility is assigned without corresponding authority.

Observable performance patterns
  • Routine matters repeatedly require senior approval
  • Decisions return to meetings without resolution
  • Project teams wait for sign-off before proceeding
  • Managers avoid taking ownership of reasonable decisions
  • Senior executives become involved in minor operational matters
Early warning signals
  • Increasing decision turnaround times
  • Growing queues of unresolved approvals
  • Recurring agenda items without closure
  • Teams repeatedly report that they are waiting for a decision
Strategic impact: Execution velocity slows because work cannot progress at the speed required. Employees lose ownership, management capacity is consumed by avoidable escalation, and the organisation becomes less responsive to customers and environmental changes.
Diagnostic question: Are decisions being made by the right people, with sufficient authority, information and speed?
Worked example

A branch manager identifies a service problem but cannot approve a small process adjustment without head-office authorisation. The delay affects customers, although the branch has the knowledge and resources to respond.

Clog 04
Misaligned Metrics & Incentives
Value Proposition
▼
Organisational performance · System reality

The performance management system measures, rewards or reinforces activities that do not adequately reflect the strategic value the organisation intends to deliver. Functional scorecards may remain unchanged after strategic priorities shift. Individual and departmental targets can encourage local optimisation while enterprise-wide outcomes deteriorate.

People performance · Contribution reality

Employees naturally pay attention to the outcomes for which they are evaluated, recognised or rewarded. When metrics are poorly aligned, people may prioritise target achievement over customer value, collaboration, quality or sustainable results. Even committed employees face tension when doing what is strategically necessary threatens their formal performance rating.

Observable performance patterns
  • Strong departmental KPI scores coexist with disappointing enterprise results
  • Teams protect their own targets at the expense of shared outcomes
  • Employees focus on measurable volume at the expense of quality or value
  • Performance discussions concentrate on score achievement without examining contribution
  • Incentives discourage collaboration or long-term improvement
Early warning signals
  • Reported performance improves while customer outcomes deteriorate
  • Departments present contradictory accounts of success
  • Targets are achieved through reclassification or selective reporting
  • Employees question why strategically valuable work receives little recognition
Strategic impact: The enterprise generates measurable activity without achieving the intended value proposition. Performance systems unintentionally reinforce behaviours that undermine the strategy, reducing both organisational effectiveness and the integrity of individual accountability.
Diagnostic question: Are people being measured and rewarded for contributions that genuinely produce the intended strategic value?
Worked example

A customer contact centre rewards agents for the number of calls completed. Agents shorten conversations to meet targets, but repeat calls increase and customer satisfaction falls.

Clog 05
Cultural Resistance
Value Proposition
▼
Organisational performance · System reality

Established norms, shared assumptions, informal rules and everyday practices do not support the behavioural changes required by the strategy. The formal strategy may demand collaboration, experimentation, customer responsiveness or accountability, while the prevailing culture reinforces hierarchy, risk avoidance, silence or functional protection.

People performance · Contribution reality

Individuals interpret the strategy through previous experiences and the behaviours they observe being rewarded or tolerated. Employees may understand the new direction but hesitate to adopt it because they do not trust its sustainability, feel unsafe challenging established practices or see leaders behaving inconsistently. Resistance can also signal unresolved concerns about feasibility, fairness or capability.

Observable performance patterns
  • Teams return to familiar practices under pressure
  • Employees comply formally without changing everyday behaviours
  • Constructive challenge and initiative remain limited
  • Cross-functional collaboration is resisted
  • Informal workplace narratives undermine formal strategic messages
Early warning signals
  • Employees remain silent during important performance discussions
  • Low participation in improvement initiatives
  • Repeated expressions of scepticism about change
  • Leaders publicly endorse behaviours they do not consistently demonstrate
Strategic impact: The organisation struggles to translate strategic intent into sustained behaviour. New systems and processes are adopted superficially, while underlying habits continue to shape delivery. Improvements are difficult to sustain because the operating culture repeatedly pulls performance back towards established practices.
Diagnostic question: Which established beliefs, norms and behaviours are reinforcing or obstructing the contributions required by the strategy?
Worked example

A company introduces a customer-first strategy, but employees are still criticised for taking initiative outside rigid procedures. Teams continue to escalate customer problems they could resolve themselves.

Clog 06
Technology / Infrastructure Lag
Competition
▼
Organisational performance · System reality

Technology, information architecture, operational infrastructure or physical assets are not sufficiently reliable, integrated or scalable to support strategic demands. Legacy platforms create fragmented information flows, weak visibility, manual intervention and constraints on service capacity. The infrastructure may also restrict the speed at which the organisation adapts to external developments.

People performance · Contribution reality

Employees spend excessive time correcting data, reconciling records, duplicating entries and finding workarounds. Managers make decisions with incomplete or outdated information. Employees may be evaluated against efficiency standards that existing systems cannot support. Repeated technology frustrations also reduce motivation and confidence in improvement initiatives.

Observable performance patterns
  • Heavy reliance on spreadsheets and manual reporting
  • Duplicate data entry across systems
  • Delayed or inconsistent management information
  • Operational errors increase with workload
  • Employees maintain unofficial systems to complete essential tasks
Early warning signals
  • Reporting cycles become progressively longer
  • Discrepancies appear between information sources
  • Routine tasks demand increasing manual effort
  • System downtime or integration failures affect critical delivery
Strategic impact: The organisation loses operational speed, information quality and the ability to scale reliably. People devote capacity to compensating for infrastructure limitations, reducing time available for value-creating work. Competitive responsiveness weakens as the enterprise struggles to match required standards of efficiency, reliability or service.
Diagnostic question: Can the organisation’s technology and infrastructure support the speed, reliability, visibility and scale required by its strategy?
Worked example

A logistics company promises real-time delivery visibility, but its transport and customer systems do not communicate. Staff compile updates manually, customers receive inconsistent information and service confidence declines.

Clog 07
Leadership Avoidance
Arena + Boundaries
▼
Organisational performance · System reality

Leadership mechanisms fail to resolve material trade-offs, repeated underperformance, resource conflicts, accountability gaps or strategic tensions. Difficult matters are discussed without binding decisions or clear follow-through. Governance and review forums become reporting rituals that leave the conditions obstructing performance unresolved.

People performance · Contribution reality

Managers and employees receive mixed signals about priorities, standards and consequences. Some learn that difficult issues can be postponed without consequence. Others become discouraged when their concerns repeatedly go unresolved. Confidence in leadership declines, and individuals may protect themselves by avoiding ownership, escalating responsibility or lowering their performance expectations.

Observable performance patterns
  • Strategic problems recur across successive reviews
  • Decisions are recorded but not consistently implemented
  • Underperformance continues without meaningful intervention
  • Leaders repeatedly change priorities without resolving trade-offs
  • Accountability is assigned without the support required to deliver
Early warning signals
  • The same issues appear in multiple performance cycles
  • Employees stop raising problems because they expect no action
  • Escalations remain unresolved despite leadership attention
  • Teams become uncertain about which priorities matter most
Strategic impact: Strategic focus weakens as unresolved issues accumulate. The organisation loses direction and delivery discipline, while people become less willing to take ownership. Over time, leadership avoidance can reinforce all six other performance clogs because their underlying causes remain unaddressed.
Diagnostic question: Are leaders making and following through on the decisions necessary to remove performance constraints?
Worked example

A management team repeatedly identifies poor coordination between sales and operations but avoids changing responsibilities because two senior executives disagree. The problem continues, and both teams are blamed for missed delivery targets.

1.4 — The Three Performance Lines of Sight from the Seven Clogs
From the result to its cause

The central discipline is to distinguish three levels of performance evidence. Each line of sight reveals a different part of the performance problem and helps leaders move from symptoms to causes.

1
Performance outcome

What result is falling short? Establish the intended strategic outcome, the actual result and the size or nature of the gap.

2
Performance behaviour

What are people or teams doing, or unable to do, that contributes to the gap? Observe specific decisions, actions, omissions, collaboration patterns and delivery practices.

3
Performance condition

What organisational arrangements, capabilities, incentives or leadership practices are causing or reinforcing that behaviour? Test the seven clogs to explain why the pattern persists.

This distinction helps leaders avoid treating every disappointing result as an individual performance failure. It also keeps people accountable for actions within their control while recognising the organisation’s responsibility for enabling conditions.

A single performance outcome can reflect several interacting clogs. The following example traces the three lines of sight before identifying the organisational conditions that require attention.

1.5 — Worked Diagnostic Example: Improving Customer Service
Tracing the three lines of sight

Enterprise Objective. Deliver a reliable and responsive customer service experience.

Enterprise Key Result. Increase customer enquiries resolved within 48 hours from 65% to 90% by year-end.

Performance figures are illustrative.

Line of Sight 1 — Performance Outcome

Only 65% of customer enquiries are resolved within 48 hours against a target of 90%. The enterprise is falling short of its intended customer responsiveness and service reliability.

Line of Sight 2 — Performance Behaviour

Service agents process enquiries and escalate cases requiring other departments. Cases are frequently transferred without end-to-end ownership, managers wait for approval on routine refunds, and staff spend their time requesting updates while customer problems wait. Employees may be achieving their activity targets while the collective resolution outcome remains weak.

Line of Sight 3 — Performance Condition

The diagnostic identifies the organisational conditions that help explain the behaviour:

40% of delayed cases require another department.Structural MisfitFragmented ownership and weak cross-functional handoffs delay resolution.
Routine refunds require senior-management approval.Decision BottlenecksPeople closest to the enquiry lack sufficient authority to resolve it.

Organisational performance interpretation

The coordination processes, decision rights, measures and information systems are not sufficiently aligned to the customer-resolution outcome. These conditions create recurring delays across the service chain.

People performance interpretation

Customer service employees may complete assigned tasks but still lack the authority, information and cross-functional support needed to resolve cases. Any individual capability or behavioural gaps must be assessed against these conditions and the standards within each person’s control.

Strategic interpretation

The customer value proposition promises responsive service, but operating arrangements do not consistently enable its delivery. The principal ABCV distortion concerns the Value Proposition, with contributing constraints in Boundaries and Competition.

Required management response

Clarify end-to-end resolution ownership, adjust routine decision authority, align measures to resolution outcomes and improve case visibility across departments. Provide coaching or accountability interventions where evidence confirms individual capability or behavioural gaps.

Closing Diagnostic Principle

Performance is produced through people operating within an organisational system. A sound diagnosis examines the result, the behaviours shaping contributions and the organisational conditions that enable or constrain them.

The 7-Clog Diagnostic Map helps leaders identify where these relationships have become misaligned, which ABCV elements are affected and what must change to restore performance.

Section 2 · Intelligence — Why

Why Disciplined Intervention Clears Strategic Clogs

Building the three disciplines that detect, classify and resolve strategic clogs, and applying the 7 Strategic Unclogging Levers across people and organisational performance.

Section learning outcomes
  • Select and apply responses to strategic clogs across people and organisational performance.
  • Intervene early in emerging execution friction before it compounds.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Intelligence

The ignition point of this section is the mindset shift: from “Why is the strategy failing?” to “Where are our people and our organisational conditions not yet ready to carry the strategy?” This diagnostic posture moves leadership from blame to engineering.

Open with the section narrative: a named clog still holds until leaders act on it. Then teach the three disciplines one at a time, each in people performance and in organisational performance: leaders detect the signals early, name the clog with the evidence of the three lines of sight, and intervene on the condition behind it.

For the 7 levers: teach what each lever changes in people performance and in organisational performance. Rarely does a single lever clear a clog alone. The diagnostic question is always: which lever combination addresses the root cause?

Section 1 traced how a strategic clog forms and how it shows: in the result that falls short, in what individuals and teams are doing or unable to do, and in the organisational conditions behind their behaviour. A named clog still holds until leaders act on it.

Leaders resolve clogs through three disciplines. They identify the clog early, while it is still a signal. They classify it accurately, so the response addresses its cause. They intervene with the lever that removes that cause. Each discipline is a habit the executive team builds through practice in every review.

Early action matters because a clog compounds. The delay it causes in one function becomes the constraint on the next, and a matter that one adjustment would have resolved early grows into a recovery effort across several functions.

Every response works on two sides at once. In people performance, it restores the authority, capability, focus and confidence individuals need to deliver their tasks. In organisational performance, it changes the structures, processes, systems, resources and governance so that they support the execution of those tasks. A response confined to one side leaves the clog in place on the other.

Part 2.1 sets out the three disciplines. Part 2.2 sets out the 7 Strategic Unclogging Levers, the interventions leaders apply once the clog is named.

Detect Signals Before They Compound

Leaders must detect the signals that indicate friction is forming. Bottlenecks, repeated coordination failures, declining execution speed, or growing ambiguity in decision rights are all early indicators that a clog is emerging.

The strategic value of early identification is significant. A clog detected when it is a friction signal requires a targeted structural adjustment. The same clog discovered six months later, after it has compounded into a systemic execution failure, requires a crisis response. The cost — in time, capital, and leadership credibility — is vastly different.

This discipline requires leaders to build signal literacy: the ability to read organisational patterns as early indicators of structural misalignment.

Early signals appear in the rhythm of work before they appear in the results. Leaders find them in three places: in the review, where the same items carry forward and explanations replace progress; in the flow of work, where hand-offs slow, approvals queue and workarounds multiply; and in conversation, where people stop raising problems or raise the same one repeatedly.

The early warning signals listed for each of the seven clogs give leaders a reference to read against. Signal literacy grows through practice: leaders ask for the signals in every review, compare them across functions and record them before the result moves.

People performance

Leaders read the signals in how individuals and teams work: decisions held for approval that the individual could take, rework rising on the same tasks, longer hours for the same output, and silence in meetings where challenge is needed. These signals appear before the KPI falls. Leaders who ask a team what is slowing its work hear them within weeks; a review that reads only the KPI sees them a quarter later.

Workforce capacity. One specialist’s queue grows each week while the projects that depend on that person report small slips.

Organisational performance

Leaders read the signals in the organisational conditions that support the execution of the individuals’ tasks.

Governance · Authority. The same unresolved item appears on three consecutive agendas, and each team waiting on it reports its delay separately.

Systems · Integration. Two reports give different figures for the same measure, and teams spend their time reconciling them.

None of these carries a number in the review, so leaders ask for them by name.

Name the Clog Precisely Before Intervening

Each clog belongs to a specific category of organisational friction. Structural misfit, capability gaps, decision bottlenecks, misaligned incentives, cultural resistance, technological constraints, or leadership avoidance each require fundamentally different forms of intervention.

The most common diagnostic failure is applying the wrong lever to a correctly identified symptom. A leader who observes “slow execution” and responds by adding resources is applying a capability lever to what may be a decision bottleneck. The additional resources do not accelerate execution because the constraint is approval latency.

Accurate classification requires resisting the impulse to solve before diagnosing. The first question is “What type of friction is this, and what does it tell us about the system?”

Classification uses the evidence of the Three Performance Lines of Sight. The outcome confirms that a clog exists. The behaviour shows how it acts on individuals and teams. The condition names the clog. Leaders test the evidence against each of the seven clogs in turn and keep the one that explains why the pattern persists.

Several clogs often act together. Leaders name the primary clog, the one whose removal releases the others, and record the contributing clogs beside it.

People performance

Leaders separate what is within the individual’s control from what the conditions impose. An individual who delays decisions may lack confidence. The same delay appears when the individual lacks the authority to decide. Classification establishes which applies, with evidence from the individual’s work, before a capability or accountability response is chosen.

Organisational performance

Leaders locate the condition behind the behaviour.

Governance · Authority. Approvals for routine work sit two levels above the people doing it. The slow delivery is a Decision Bottleneck.

Systems · Integration. The hand-offs between three functions have no single owner. The same slow delivery is Structural Misfit.

One symptom leads to two different clogs, and to two different levers. Classification decides which one the team pulls.

Pull the Right Lever with Precision

Once the clog is identified and classified, it must be addressed through the appropriate strategic lever. Clogs rarely clear themselves through trying harder or working more hours. They require a deliberate adjustment to the organisation’s structural, behavioural, and operational settings.

Targeted intervention means matching the lever to the root cause. It also means accepting that rarely does a single clog require just one fix. A Capability Gap may require both Upgrade (systems) and Reinforce (skills). A Leadership Avoidance clog may require both Clarify (decision rights) and Rebuild Trust (credibility restoration).

A targeted intervention names what will change, who will change it and the evidence that will show the clog clearing. Leaders set that evidence before they act: the signal they expect to fall, the behaviour they expect to change and the result they expect to move.

Leaders also sequence the response. Where two levers are needed, the lever that changes the condition usually comes first, and the lever that builds the people’s capability or confidence follows, so that individuals apply new skills in conditions that support them.

People performance

The intervention equips individuals to deliver their tasks under the changed conditions: clear authority, the skills the strategy now requires, coaching through the first cycles and consistent consequences. Leaders confirm the change in the individuals’ work: decisions taken at the right level, less rework and problems raised early.

Workforce capacity. Specialist capacity is added where the strategy concentrates demand.

Organisational performance

The intervention changes the organisational condition behind the clog.

Governance · Authority. Delegation limits for routine decisions are reset and published.

Systems · Integration. One source of customer data replaces three, and every report draws on it.

Leaders confirm the change in the condition itself, then in the Key Result.

The goal is to develop a leadership rhythm that moves quickly and precisely: Identify the Clog → Select the Lever → Targeted Intervention.

2.2 — The 7 Strategic Unclogging Levers
Select each lever to explore

Think of these seven levers as the leadership toolkit for execution architecture. They restore the conditions that allow the strategy to move through the organisation at the speed and quality intended. Each lever targets a specific dimension of the system.

Lever 01
Standardise
Ensure Consistency
▼

Harmonises processes and service levels across teams so everyone plays by the same rules.

Variability is a silent killer of speed. By establishing a shared “way we work,” you reduce duplication and ensure every unit pulls in the same direction. Apply when: Variability across teams is creating drag. Best applied to: Structural Misfit, Misaligned Metrics & Incentives, Technology / Infrastructure Lag.

How it works. Standardise sets one agreed way of working where the strategy needs consistent results across teams, sites or channels. Leaders define the standard, the measure that shows it is met and the leader responsible for keeping it current. The standard covers the work that must be consistent and leaves local judgement where the strategy needs it.

People performance

Individuals work to one set of expectations, so effort is no longer lost translating between teams. Leaders train every team on the standard and hold each individual to the same measure of quality and timeliness.

Organisational performance

Systems · Standardisation. Three regions run three versions of customer onboarding. One process replaces them, with one set of service levels.

Governance · Policies & Procedures. A named leader maintains the standard and reviews compliance each quarter.

Lever 02
Streamline
Increase Speed
▼

Simplifies workflows and removes the red tape layers that add no value to execution.

Leaner processes mean faster approvals and a team that can pivot as quickly as the market demands. Apply when: Approval processes are creating latency. Best applied to: Decision Bottlenecks, Structural Misfit.

How it works. Streamline removes the steps, approvals and hand-offs that add time without adding control or value. Leaders map the path a piece of work takes, mark each point where it waits, and remove or combine the steps that change nothing in the outcome. Controls that protect the organisation stay, placed at the point of greatest risk.

People performance

Individuals spend their time on the task itself. Managers decide within clear limits, teams stop chasing approvals, and work moves at the pace the strategy requires.

Organisational performance

Governance · Authority. Routine purchases below an agreed limit no longer need senior approval.

Systems · Process Efficiency. One electronic approval route replaces email chains, and every request shows where it sits.

Lever 03
Clarify
Eliminate Confusion
▼

Defines exactly who owns what, where the handoffs are, and who has the final say.

Ambiguity breeds hesitation. When people know their roles and decision rights, accountability returns and work stops getting stuck in limbo. Apply when: Ownership ambiguity is causing hesitation. Best applied to: Decision Bottlenecks, Structural Misfit, Leadership Avoidance.

How it works. Clarify settles who owns each outcome, who decides, who must be consulted and where work passes from one team to the next. Leaders write the decision rights and hand-offs down, agree them across the functions involved and communicate them to everyone who works across the boundary.

People performance

Individuals know which decisions are theirs and act on them. Escalation falls, ownership returns to the level closest to the work, and accountability can be applied fairly because responsibility now carries matching authority.

Organisational performance

Governance · Authority. One executive owns the end-to-end customer-resolution outcome across service, operations and finance.

Governance · Authority. Each open trade-off has a named decision-maker and a date by which the decision is made.

Lever 04
Upgrade
Remove Bottlenecks
▼

Fixes underperforming technology and infrastructure that holds execution back.

You cannot run a modern strategy on yesterday’s tools. Upgrading removes the physical and digital friction that makes scaling feel impossible. Apply when: Systems are creating friction that blocks flow. Best applied to: Technology / Infrastructure Lag, Capability Gaps.

How it works. Upgrade replaces or improves the technology, infrastructure or capability that cannot carry the strategy’s demands. Leaders define the performance the strategy requires in speed, reliability, visibility and scale, measure the gap and invest where the gap constrains the Key Result most.

People performance

Individuals stop spending their time on workarounds: re-keying data, reconciling records and building reports by hand. That time returns to the work the strategy needs, and confidence in improvement initiatives recovers.

Organisational performance

Systems · Integration. The customer and transport systems are connected, so delivery status updates without manual entry.

Environment · Infrastructure. Field teams receive reliable connectivity, so orders reach operations the same day.

Lever 05
Reinforce
Strengthen Behaviour
▼

Equips the team with the skills, coaching, and confidence they need to actually deliver.

Strategy requires new behaviours. Reinforcement ensures your people are capable of making the change. Apply when: Skills or confidence are the limiting factor. Best applied to: Capability Gaps, Cultural Resistance.

How it works. Reinforce builds the skills, habits and confidence the strategy requires of individuals and teams. Leaders identify the behaviours the strategy depends on, provide training and coaching, model the behaviours themselves and recognise them when they appear.

People performance

Individuals gain the capability to meet the new expectations and the confidence to apply it. Coaching through the first cycles turns new skills into habits, and recognition shows people that the new behaviours count.

Values and consequences. Leaders’ own decisions and recognition reflect the behaviours the strategy asks for.

Organisational performance

Resources · Investment. A development programme in digital credit assessment is funded for the whole lending team.

Environment · Technology. Lending officers receive the credit-assessment software the training uses, so new skills are applied on live cases from the first week.

Lever 06
Realign
Focus on Results
▼

Adjusts metrics and rewards so they actually match current strategic priorities.

People do what they are measured on. When you fix the incentives, the team’s energy naturally shifts from protecting their patch to creating enterprise value. Apply when: Effort is flowing only to what is measured. Best applied to: Misaligned Metrics & Incentives.

How it works. Realign changes measures, targets and rewards so that they reinforce the contributions the strategy needs. Leaders trace each scorecard measure to the Key Result it serves, remove or reweight the measures that reward local results at the expense of the enterprise outcome, and add measures for shared results.

People performance

Individuals no longer have to choose between their rating and the strategy. Collaboration, quality and contribution to shared outcomes are recognised, so committed people can do what the strategy requires without personal cost.

Organisational performance

Governance · Policies & Procedures. Sales bonuses are paid on margin and customer retention alongside volume.

Systems · Integration. Shared enterprise measures appear on every functional scorecard that contributes to them.

Lever 07
Rebuild Trust
Restore Credibility
▼

Heals relationships and introduces the transparency needed for people to commit again.

Trust is the social grease of execution. Without it, the system seizes up. Rebuilding it restores the psychological safety required for high-stakes work. Apply when: Psychological safety or leadership credibility is the constraint. Best applied to: Cultural Resistance, Leadership Avoidance.

How it works. Rebuild Trust restores the credibility of leadership and the safety people need to speak, challenge and commit. Leaders acknowledge what has gone wrong, act on the concerns people raise, follow through on their decisions and make the reasons for those decisions visible.

People performance

Individuals raise problems early again, offer ideas and commit to decisions they helped shape. Strong performers regain confidence in the direction.

Psychological safety. Leaders open each review by asking what is blocking delivery, and act on what they hear.

Organisational performance

Governance · Policies & Procedures. Commitments missed at senior level are addressed to the same standard applied elsewhere.

Governance · Risk Management. Delivery risks raised in a review are logged with an owner and a date, and the log is examined at the next review.

◆ Lever Selection Principle

Identifying a clog starts the work. Lever selection completes it. Rarely does a clog require just one fix. If the clog is a Capability Gap, the leaders may need to Upgrade the systems while simultaneously Reinforcing the team’s skills. If the clog is Leadership Avoidance, there will likely be a need to Clarify decision rights and Rebuild Trust to move forward. Always ask: “Which lever combination addresses the root cause?”

4.1 — Normalising the Unclog: From Crisis to Habit
The collective discipline

Performance friction is a normal feature of organisational life. Strategic priorities shift, resources become constrained, dependencies increase and existing operating arrangements may no longer support the required results. Without a shared leadership discipline, these problems are discussed informally, addressed within silos or left unresolved across successive performance reviews.

Normalising the unclog means making the early detection and removal of performance constraints part of how the executive team operates. Leaders raise concerns through evidence, examine the contributing conditions together and agree on an accountable response. Surfacing friction is treated as a contribution to organisational learning and performance, including when the condition involves another executive portfolio.

The leadership team follows a consistent path: REVIEW the performance evidence and confirm the clog; REALIGN executive ownership and the changes required across people and organisational performance; RESTORE by activating the relevant Seven Unclogging Lever and verifying recovery.

Review–Realign–Restore is the cycle every performance review follows. Here the executive team applies it together, as the Unclogging Cycle, to a clog that crosses executive portfolios.

Collective leadership principle. Every executive is responsible for making emerging friction visible. The leaders who control the contributing conditions must act together to remove it, with clear individual ownership for each agreed commitment.

4.2 — The Unclogging Cycle: Review–Realign–Restore
The S2R® discipline · Three stages, one record

The Unclogging Cycle is the S2R® discipline the executive team follows to remove a clog. REVIEW establishes the performance reality and confirms the clog. REALIGN settles which leaders act and what must change. RESTORE activates the Unclogging Lever and verifies recovery. Each stage completes one part of the Unclogging Record, so the record the team holds at the end is the evidence of the whole cycle.

REVIEW — Establish the Performance Reality

REVIEW establishes a shared, evidence-based understanding of the result falling short and the organisational condition contributing to it. Leaders use the Three Performance Lines of Sight to distinguish the outcome from the behaviours observed and the conditions causing or reinforcing them.

  1. Performance Outcome — Confirm the Key Result or KPI that is underperforming and establish the actual gap.
  2. Performance Behaviour — Identify what individuals or teams are doing, delaying or unable to do that contributes to the gap.
  3. Performance Condition — Test the evidence against the Seven Clogs and identify the condition sustaining the behaviour.

The review conversation must allow leaders to surface performance friction across functional boundaries. Concerns should be examined through observable evidence, including where the condition may sit within another executive portfolio.

People performance

REVIEW examines what individuals and teams are doing, delaying or unable to do, and separates what is within their control from what the conditions impose. Leaders draw the evidence from the work itself: decisions waiting, hand-offs repeated, rework recorded.

Organisational performance

REVIEW tests whether the organisational conditions support the execution of the individuals’ tasks, and locates the condition sustaining the behaviour. Leaders examine the evidence across executive portfolios, because the condition often sits outside the function where the clog shows.

REVIEW is complete when the executive team agrees on the performance gap, the supporting evidence and the clog requiring intervention.
Into the Unclogging Record: the performance gap, the evidence from the three lines of sight and the confirmed clog.
REALIGN — Establish Collective Executive Ownership

Once a performance clog has been confirmed, the executive team must determine which organisational condition needs to change and which leaders have the authority and responsibility to act. A clog may be experienced in one function but sustained by a decision or operating arrangement in another.

Three Leadership Decisions
  1. What must change? Identify the specific organisational arrangement, practice or decision sustaining the clog.
  2. Who must act together? Appoint one coordinating executive owner and name the contributing executives needed to resolve the condition.
  3. What must be agreed? Establish the decisions, responsibilities, resources and timelines required for the intervention.

Before finalising the response, leaders consider the implications for both dimensions of performance:

People performance

What must change in expectations, authority, capabilities, behaviours or support so individuals and teams can deliver their contributions?

The answer becomes the commitments made to the people delivering the result: the authority they receive, the support they are given and what is expected of them.

Organisational performance

What must change in structures, processes, systems, resources, decision rights or governance so the enterprise can produce the intended result?

The answer becomes the organisational change the executives agree, with the leader responsible for each part.

The agreed intervention should address the confirmed organisational condition while enabling the people responsible for delivery to perform effectively.

Every executive has a responsibility to recognise and surface performance friction. Collective ownership is supported by clear individual accountability: one leader coordinates the resolution, while other executives remain accountable for their agreed contributions.

REALIGN is complete when the relevant leaders have agreed what must change, who will act and what each executive must deliver.
Into the Unclogging Record: the coordinating executive, the contributing executives and the agreed changes for people and organisational performance.

The Seven Unclogging Levers provide the established S2R® interventions for addressing the conditions represented by the Seven Performance Clogs. 4.3 — Worked Example: The Unclogging Cycle
A manufacturing case

Enterprise Objective. Improve production reliability and on-time customer delivery.

Enterprise Key Result. Increase customer orders delivered on time from 72% to 95% by year-end.

Performance figures are illustrative. The executive team notices that production interruptions are contributing to late deliveries.

Step 1 — REVIEW: Confirm the Performance Clog

Identified clog. Decision Bottlenecks.

The team confirms that avoidable approval delays contribute to maintenance downtime and late customer deliveries.

Step 2 — REALIGN: Agree on Collective Ownership

The CFO and COO agree that routine maintenance decisions must be faster while appropriate financial controls remain in place.

Maintenance managers gain clear authority to purchase approved routine replacement parts and can act promptly within defined controls.

Organisational performance implication

The approval system preserves financial oversight while enabling reliable equipment maintenance and uninterrupted production flow.

Step 3 — RESTORE: Activate the Unclogging Lever

Selected lever. Lever 02 — Streamline | Increase Speed.

Streamline simplifies workflows and removes approval layers that add no value to execution. It is appropriate because the confirmed clog is Decision Bottlenecks, and routine maintenance purchases are delayed by unnecessary approval steps.

Activation. The CFO and COO implement the following changes:

  1. Identify approval steps that cause avoidable delays to routine maintenance purchases.
  2. Remove redundant approvals and simplify the routine purchase workflow.
  3. Retain appropriate controls and senior approval for exceptional or higher-risk expenditure.
  4. Communicate and implement the streamlined process with maintenance and operational teams.
People performance after Streamline

Maintenance managers can obtain routine replacement parts promptly and fulfil their responsibilities within clear approval limits, without repeated unnecessary escalation.

Organisational performance after Streamline

Reduced approval time lowers maintenance downtime, supports production continuity and helps restore on-time customer delivery while preserving financial control.

Verification. At the agreed executive review, examine:

  • Routine maintenance approval turnaround time.
  • Equipment downtime caused by delayed replacement parts.
  • On-time customer delivery performance.
  • Compliance with the revised financial controls.

The intervention is working when maintenance managers can exercise the revised authority confidently, routine decisions are made faster, approval-related downtime falls and customer delivery performance begins to recover without weakening financial control.

Section 5 · Application — In Practice

The Strategic Unclogging Clinic

Returning to an original work-based challenge, identifying the performance clog, selecting the corresponding Strategic Unclogging Lever and applying Review–Realign–Restore within the Capstone organisation.

Section learning outcomes
  • Diagnose a Capstone challenge using the Seven Performance Clogs and select a suitable Unclogging Lever.

◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — The Strategic Unclogging Clinic

In its Problem Charter, each group recorded recurring work-based organisational challenges drawn from its members’ professional experiences and chose the challenges its executive team would address. These challenges informed the simulated Capstone organisation. Participants now return to that list, select one challenge and apply the S2R® diagnostic and intervention tools they have learned.

Teach the sequence clearly: original challenge → identify the clog → select the lever → Review–Realign–Restore. The initial classification is a working diagnosis; REVIEW checks it against performance evidence before the team commits to action. If REVIEW reveals a different underlying condition, the team must correct the clog and lever selection.

Teach the group to support its diagnosis: the group explains why its selected clog fits the observed behaviours and organisational conditions. During REALIGN, the group sets out the implications for both people and organisational performance. During RESTORE, the group specifies the application of the selected lever, named executive ownership, completion timing and evidence of recovery.

  4. The group chooses its scribe for this work. The scribe types what the group agrees, and the entries appear on every member’s page.
  5. In part 5.6 each member reads the group’s record and selects Agree. When every member has agreed, the record feeds the team’s Capstone S2R® Blueprint.

5.5 — Diagnostic Quality Check
Before the response is finalised

Before the group finalises its response, it tests the response against four criteria:

  1. The original challenge is linked to a specific strategic result and supported by realistic performance evidence.
  2. The selected clog names the underlying organisational condition behind the symptom.
  3. The lever addresses the confirmed clog, and REALIGN considers both people and organisational performance.
  4. The RESTORE intervention is specific, owned, time-bound and supported by evidence that will show whether performance is recovering.

If any criterion is not met, the group returns to the relevant step and refines the response.

Unit Summary
Unit 9 synthesis
Awareness — What

A Strategic Clog is an internal blockage that prevents strategic intent from flowing into execution. Clogs form where the strategy stretches today’s organisation. Performance has two connected dimensions, organisational performance and people performance. The 7-Clog Diagnostic Map, aligned to ABCV Directional Integrity, examines both, and the Three Performance Lines of Sight trace a result from its outcome to the behaviours and the conditions behind it.

Intelligence — Why

Leaders resolve clogs through three disciplines: early identification, accurate classification and targeted intervention. The 7 Strategic Unclogging Levers act on people performance and organisational performance together.

Extrapolating — Where

Every executive function shapes the conditions in which other teams work. Traced through the Three Performance Lines of Sight, a decision or practice in one function can create or reinforce a clog in another.

Integration — Collective

Clogs rarely stay in one lane. The leadership team removes them together through the Unclogging Cycle: REVIEW confirms the clog, REALIGN agrees collective ownership and the changes for people and organisational performance, and RESTORE activates the Unclogging Lever and verifies recovery. Each stage completes part of the Unclogging Record.

Application — In Practice

Each group selected a challenge from its Problem Charter, identified the clog, selected the lever and simulated the Unclogging Cycle within its Capstone organisation. The agreed record feeds the team’s Capstone S2R® Blueprint.

“When unclogging becomes a habit, everything changes. Friction is a signal telling you how the organisation needs to evolve next. You start engineering flow.”

◆ Facilitator Closing Note

Close by asking each leader to name one clog that has been present in their organisation for more than six months — visible, discussed, but not yet cleared. Ask them: “What has it actually cost the organisation in strategic momentum? And what is the real reason it hasn’t been cleared yet?” The answers to the second question are almost always the true clogs beneath the clogs. That conversation is the beginning of genuine execution engineering. Remind participants that the group’s confirmed Section 5 record feeds the team’s Capstone S2R® Blueprint.
