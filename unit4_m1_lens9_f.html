Module 4 · Grounding · Unit 10

ESRG Alignment

Grounding · Pillar 3 of DIG

Grounding establishes the organisational capabilities and healthy operating status required to anchor and sustain strategic performance. It considers how the enterprise is configured to deliver results, the organisational health that sustains performance, and how both are leveraged over time.

The Grounding learning journey progresses from Unit 10 — ESRG Alignment (organisational operating environment), to Unit 11 — Culture Reinforcement & Organisational Health (culture), and then to Unit 12 — Sustaining Performance, which leverages both to sustain high performance.

Module learning outcomes Configure organisational operating conditions to support strategic priorities and enable consistent enterprise performance. Shape organisational culture by establishing and embedding the conditions and practices that strengthen organisational health. Integrate organisational capabilities and cultural practices into a coherent operating environment that supports sustained high performance. Institutionalise organisational performance sustainability through mechanisms that preserve strategic alignment, strengthen adaptability and maintain performance continuity.
Key learning outcomes Design an ESRG-aligned organisational operating environment by establishing the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance requirements necessary to deliver strategic commitments. Develop targeted organisational alignment interventions that address operating constraints and strengthen the organisation’s capacity for consistent strategic delivery.

Section 1 · Awareness — What

What operating conditions must support strategic delivery?

Specifying the organisational capability one Objective requires, domain by domain.

Section learning outcomes
  • Specify the essential organisational operating requirements associated with a selected Objective.
  • Assign each requirement to the appropriate ESRG domain and component, explaining its direct contribution to the intended result.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Awareness

Begin with one Objective from the capstone. Make clear that the ESRG review works at the level of the Objective, once a year, and that Key Results are reviewed in EPIC. Ask: “What must be in place within the organisation for this result to be delivered?” Recall the four ESRG domains and their three components each from Unit 6. Ask participants to identify only the operating requirements that materially affect their chosen commitment. At this stage, the task is to define and locate requirements; detailed evidence testing follows in Intelligence. When comparing ESRG Alignment with Strategic Unclogging, make clear that ESRG conditions are scored once a year, at the ESRG Annual Scan, and the clogs that arise between reviews are cleared through Strategic Unclogging.

ESRG Alignment — The Grounding Question

In Unit 6 participants met ESRG as the Organisational Operating System: Environment (FIT), Systems (SIP), Resources (AIR) and Governance (PAR), each with three components. There, ESRG answered a performance management question: when results fall short, does the cause lie in people’s performance or in the operating conditions around them?

Unit 10 builds on that foundation. It uses ESRG to ground performance at the level of the Objective: before demand arrives, leaders specify what each component must provide for an Objective to be delivered, and build that capability deliberately. Key Results are reviewed through the year in EPIC (Unit 8); the ESRG review sits one level above them, on the Objective those Key Results serve. Parts 1.1 to 1.4 take each domain in turn and set out, component by component, what must be established for strategic delivery. The executive team tests these requirements once a year, at the ESRG Annual Scan of each Objective.

ESRG Alignment and Strategic Unclogging: When to Use Each

In Unit 9 participants used Strategic Unclogging to clear clogs: blockages that slow a Key Result already in execution. They read each clog through its performance outcome, the behaviour around it and the condition behind it, across people performance and organisational performance. They cleared it with a Strategic Unclogging Lever and the Review–Realign–Restore cycle.

ESRG Alignment works on the organisational operating conditions themselves. It starts from an Objective and the demand that Objective places on the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance. Its purpose is to build the organisational capability that carries the strategy. Leaders specify the conditions before demand arrives, score them once a year, at the ESRG Annual Scan, and strengthen them through targeted organisational alignment interventions. Between annual reviews, the clogs that appear in execution are cleared through Strategic Unclogging, so the operating conditions are not rescored every quarter.

The two disciplines feed each other in three ways:

  • When EPIC or Strategic Unclogging traces a Key Result’s shortfall to an organisational condition, that condition becomes an ESRG component to score and strengthen at the next ESRG Annual Scan of its Objective.
  • Recurring clogs in the same component show a capability gap the next ESRG Annual Scan must address.
  • A component that scores low at the ESRG Annual Scan shows leaders where clogs are likely to form during the year, so they can watch for them in EPIC and act before Key Results slow.

Illustration: During EPIC, a service team’s response-time Key Result shows it has slipped for three weeks because cases wait between the sales and operations platforms. Strategic Unclogging clears the backlog: leaders realign the handover and restore the response time. At the ESRG Annual Scan of the Objective that Key Result serves, ESRG Alignment shows the same low Integration score in three functions. The executive team then agrees a shared integration requirement so the clog does not return.

1.1 — Environment | FIT

Environment concerns the suitability and reliability of the physical and technological setting in which organisational operations take place.

FITComponentWhat to establish for strategic delivery
FFacilitiesWhether operating locations and physical facilities are functional and suited to the planned activity.
IInfrastructureWhether essential utilities, connectivity and supporting infrastructure can reliably carry operational demand.
TTechnologyWhether the technological operating environment is available, reliable and fit for the required activity.

Diagnostic question: Are our facilities, infrastructure and technology fit for the strategic requirement?

Illustration: A production expansion depends on stable electrical supply. Repeated outages prevent the additional production capacity from being used consistently.

Diagnostic question: Are our operational processes standardised, integrated and efficient?

Illustration: Customer onboarding follows a documented process, but disconnected sales and finance platforms require repeated manual entry, slowing completion.

Diagnostic question: Are our assets and financial resources sufficient and effectively utilised?

Illustration: A logistics operation has enough vehicles, but uneven deployment leaves some idle while others are overused, reducing delivery reliability.

Diagnostic question: Is our organisational governance up to PAR?

Illustration: Funding for expansion has been approved, yet avoidable approval levels delay the release of expenditure and postpone implementation.

Section 2 · Intelligence — Why

The ESRG Ignition Point: How Each Domain Enables Execution

The four execution-enabling capabilities, and the ESRG Annual Scan through which an executive team reads its operating conditions at three levels of intelligence.

Section learning outcomes
  • Translate strategic performance requirements into functional operating capabilities through the four ESRG execution enablers.
  • Distinguish convergence, divergence and dependency in organisational operating conditions to determine the collective response each requires.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Intelligence

In Awareness, participants specified the operating conditions their strategic commitments require and located them within FIT, SIP, AIR and PAR. Intelligence now asks whether those conditions enable the level of performance demanded. Introduce the four execution-enabling capabilities, then the ESRG Annual Scan and the three levels of intelligence it moves through: Functional Intelligence (Convergence and Divergence within each function), Cross-Functional Intelligence (overlapping interventions and dependencies between functions) and Enterprise Intelligence (impact on enterprise results). The Functional ESRG Performance Scanner, completed once a year in the ESRG Annual Scan, is taught through the worked example in Integration. Detailed root-cause diagnosis belongs in Extrapolating.

2.1 — The ESRG Ignition Point
◆ Facilitator prompt

Once 2.1 is taught, ask: Which of the four capabilities is most critical to the Objective your function serves, and what would happen if it were unable to meet the required demand?

The ESRG Ignition Point is reached when organisational operating conditions are sufficiently aligned to support the performance demands of the strategy. The four domains make distinct, interdependent contributions to execution:

ESRG domainExecution-enabling capabilityWhat it makes possible
Environment · FITOperational ReadinessProvides a dependable operating setting: facilities accommodate planned activity; essential infrastructure sustains continuity; and technology has the functionality, reliability and capacity to handle strategic demand. This reduces interruptions and protects service or production availability.
Systems · SIPOperational FlowEnables work to progress predictably from initiation to completion: standardised processes create consistency, integrated workflows support handovers across functions, and efficient process design removes avoidable steps, duplication and waiting time. This improves throughput and turnaround time.
Resources · AIRDelivery CapacityMakes the required scale of delivery achievable: productive assets are available when needed, financial investment supports strategic priorities, and existing resources are deployed where demand is greatest. This prevents capacity shortages and avoidable underutilisation.
Governance · PARDecision ResponsivenessAllows organisational action to proceed with clarity and control: policies and procedures guide consistent operations, authority arrangements enable decisions at the right level and time, and risks are identified and managed. This reduces approval bottlenecks while protecting sound decisions.

Ignition principle: A strategic commitment can be delivered consistently only when the operating conditions it depends upon can meet its performance demands.

Illustration: A customer service function has reliable technology and adequate assets, but disconnected handovers delay case resolution. Its constraint is Operational Flow (SIP). Additional equipment would not directly resolve the delay.

2.2 — Three Dimensions of Collective ESRG Intelligence

The ESRG Ignition Point in 2.1 is reached for the enterprise only when each execution-enabling capability holds across every function an Objective depends on. Leaders test this once a year through the ESRG Annual Scan.

The ESRG Annual Scan is the leadership team’s yearly reading of the operating conditions that ground performance. Once a year, each function scores the components of Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance as they stand in its own environment, at Objective level, using the Functional ESRG Performance Scanner taught through the worked example in Section 4. Key Results are reviewed through EPIC across the year. The executive team then brings the functional readings together and agrees the interventions that will carry the organisation’s performance through the year ahead.

The scan moves through three levels of intelligence:

Functional Intelligence

Each function reads its own scores. The pattern among its members’ scores shows whether the function holds one view of a component or different views, and the response discipline sets how the function acts on it.

Cross-Functional Intelligence

The functions bring their maps together to combine overlapping interventions and settle where one function relies on another.

Enterprise Intelligence

The executive team weighs every intervention against its impact on enterprise results and sets the order of delivery.

How the three levels of intelligence work in the scan

The scan builds intelligence in three levels. Each level works from the output of the level before it.

Functional Intelligence: reading each function from the inside. Each member of a function scores the 12 components on their own. The function then lays its members’ scores side by side, component by component.

  • Where members score a component alike, the function holds one view of it. A shared low score calls for one coordinated response, led by one accountable person. A shared high score confirms a strength the function protects.
  • Where members score a component very differently, the function tests the evidence behind each score and agrees the actual requirement before it acts.

Each function leaves this level with its own map of interventions.

Cross-Functional Intelligence: reading the functions against each other. The functions bring their maps together. Two things show up only at this level:

  • Overlapping interventions. These are combined into one, so the organisation funds and leads each of them once.
  • Dependencies, where one function’s delivery relies on a capability another function supplies or controls. For each dependency, the functions agree the shared requirement, the order of action and what each commits to the other.

The functions leave this level with one consolidated set of interventions.

Enterprise Intelligence: reading the interventions against the strategy. The executive team weighs each intervention against its impact on enterprise results, meaning the Objective and its Key Results, and sets the order of delivery. The prioritised list is the ESRG Alignment Key: the operating conditions the organisation commits to strengthen in the year ahead.

Section 3 · Extrapolating — Where

Where Do Executive Biases Distort ESRG Operating Capabilities?

Examining how executive assumptions, functional priorities and decision-making practices influence organisational operating conditions, create execution constraints and affect strategic performance.

Section learning outcomes
  • Diagnose executive decision biases that distort specific ESRG components and weaken the organisational capabilities required for strategic execution.
  • Develop evidence-based debiasing techniques that strengthen executive decision-making and prevent cross-functional operating constraints.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Extrapolating

Connect this section to the four execution-enabling capabilities introduced in Intelligence: Environment — Operational Readiness; Systems — Operational Flow; Resources — Delivery Capacity; Governance — Decision Responsiveness.

Executives influence these capabilities through decisions within their functional portfolios. Legitimate functional priorities can introduce assumptions that affect operating conditions beyond the executive’s immediate portfolio. A decision intended to improve one function may inadvertently constrain another.

For example, a CFO may reduce expenditure to protect financial performance while weakening investment required for operational continuity. A COO may standardise procedures to improve consistency while introducing unnecessary delays.

Central question: Where might my executive decisions and assumptions be weakening the organisational operating capabilities required to deliver our strategy?

◆ Facilitator Approach

For each CXO card: introduce the bias and decision; examine the two ESRG components most directly affected; trace the execution consequences; and discuss the evidence or decision adjustment required to counter the bias. Invite participants to challenge the examples and identify alternative decisions that preserve functional objectives while strengthening enterprise performance. Examples are illustrative.

Section 4 · Integration — Collective

How Does the Executive Team Create Collective Intelligence for ESRG Alignment?

A worked example of the ESRG Annual Scan in four steps: the members of each function score its operating conditions, each function reads its pattern, the functions combine and align their interventions, and the executive team prioritises them.

Section learning outcomes
  • Integrate the functional ESRG readings of an executive team into one coherent set of interventions that strengthens cross-functional delivery.
  • Prioritise operating-condition interventions by their impact on enterprise results.
◆ FACILITATOR GUIDANCE — Integration

Integration is the worked example of the ESRG Annual Scan, in four steps: the members of each function score its operating conditions (4.1); each function reads its pattern and agrees its interventions, which is Functional Intelligence (4.2); the functions combine overlapping interventions and align their dependencies, which is Cross-Functional Intelligence (4.3); and the executive team prioritises the interventions by their impact on enterprise results, which is Enterprise Intelligence (4.4). Take participants through the steps in order. Participants do not rescore the illustrative data. In Section 5, each group carries out the same four steps for two functions of its own company.

Central question: What can the executive team discover about the operating environment collectively that no function can establish from its own assessment alone?

The worked example: One illustrative company carries out its ESRG Annual Scan; the company and its figures come from no real organisation. Its Objective is to be the most reliable supplier in its market. One of its Key Results, fulfilling customer orders within five working days, is reviewed through the year in EPIC; once a year, the executive team scans ESRG for the Objective. The example follows two of the functions that carry the Objective, Operations and Commercial, each scored by three of its members.

The scan runs in four steps: Step 1, the members of each function score its operating conditions (4.1); Step 2, each function reads its pattern and agrees its interventions (4.2, Functional Intelligence); Step 3, the functions combine overlapping interventions and align their dependencies (4.3, Cross-Functional Intelligence); Step 4, the executive team prioritises the interventions by their impact on enterprise results (4.4, Enterprise Intelligence).

4.2 — Step 2: Functional Intelligence: Each Function Reads Its Pattern
◆ Facilitator prompt

Once 4.2 is taught, ask: Why might members of the same function score one component very differently, and what evidence would settle it?

Each function places its members’ scores side by side, component by component. Scores are alike when every member’s score lies within one point of the others; they are very different when two members’ scores lie two or more points apart. Convergence on a low score calls for Coordinate: one response for the function, led by one accountable person. Convergence on a high score confirms a strength the function protects. Divergence calls for Reconcile: the function tests the evidence behind each score, establishes whether the gap lies in the condition or in how it was scored, and agrees the actual requirement.

The unit concept

A strategic commitment can be delivered consistently only when the operating conditions it depends on can meet its performance demands. ESRG Alignment grounds organisational performance at the level of the Objective: before demand arrives, leaders specify what each component of the Environment, Systems, Resources and Governance must provide, test it once a year in the ESRG Annual Scan, and build the capability deliberately. Each domain enables execution through one capability: Operational Readiness, Operational Flow, Delivery Capacity and Decision Responsiveness. Each function reads these capabilities from its own position: its members’ scores show where they see a component alike or very differently, the functions combine and align their interventions, and the executive team prioritises the interventions by their impact on enterprise results.

3. The ESRG Ignition Point

The Ignition Point is reached when operating conditions are aligned enough to support the performance demands of the strategy. Each domain makes a distinct, interdependent contribution:

DomainExecution-enabling capabilityWhat it makes possible
Environment · FITOperational ReadinessA dependable operating setting that reduces interruptions and protects service or production availability
Systems · SIPOperational FlowWork that progresses predictably from initiation to completion, improving throughput and turnaround
Resources · AIRDelivery CapacityThe required scale of delivery, preventing capacity shortages and avoidable underutilisation
Governance · PARDecision ResponsivenessAction that proceeds with clarity and control, reducing approval bottlenecks while protecting sound decisions

Ignition principle: a strategic commitment can be delivered consistently only when the operating conditions it depends on can meet its performance demands. An intervention must address the capability that is actually constraining delivery.

4. The three levels of intelligence in the ESRG Annual Scan

The ESRG Annual Scan is the leadership team’s yearly reading of the operating conditions that ground performance, at Objective level; Key Results are reviewed through EPIC across the year. Each function scores the components as they stand in its own environment; the executive team then brings the functional readings together and agrees the interventions for the year ahead.

A difference in scores does not by itself prove a difference in condition, and a shared low score does not by itself prove a shared cause: the evidence is tested before the response is agreed.

5. The ESRG Annual Scan

  1. The members of each function score its operating conditions. Each member scores the 12 components for the Objective on their own, from evidence of how policies, systems and plans perform in practice, on a 1–5 scale: 1 Critical constraint (materially prevents required performance); 2 Significant gap (regularly disrupts or limits performance); 3 Partially effective (supports basic operations, not strategic demand); 4 Effective (reliably supports current requirements); 5 Fully aligned (supports current and anticipated demand).
  2. Each function reads its pattern (Functional Intelligence). Scores within one point of each other are alike (Convergence); scores two or more points apart are very different (Divergence). Convergence on a low score calls for Coordinate; Divergence calls for Reconcile.
  3. The functions combine and align (Cross-Functional Intelligence). Overlapping interventions are combined into one; where one function relies on a capability another supplies or controls, the functions agree the shared operating requirement, the order of action and what each commits to the other.
  4. The executive team builds its ESRG Alignment Key (Enterprise Intelligence). It prioritises the interventions by their impact on enterprise results and names the lead executive for each. The Key is the commitment for the year.
